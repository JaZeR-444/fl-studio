# Genre Rules: Hip-Hop & Breakbeat - Fruity Slicer 2

## Hip-Hop/Boom Bap Slicing Strategy

### Core Philosophy
**Chop classic breaks, rearrange for bounce - swing and grit are essential**

---

### Classic Breakbeat Slicing

**Slice Division:** 1/16 notes (most common)

**Why:**
- Captures every hit in complex breaks
- Enough granularity for creative rearrangement
- Standard for Amen, Apache, Funky Drummer, etc.

**Workflow:**
1. Load classic break (Amen Break, Think Break, etc.)
2. Set BPM (usually 130-180 for original breaks)
3. Auto-Slice @ 1/16 division
4. Fine-tune slice markers manually (isolate kick, snare)

---

### Boom Bap Drum Slicing

**Slice Strategy:** Isolate key hits (kick, snare, hi-hat)

**Configuration:**
- **Kick slices:** Short decay (tight), HP at 50 Hz (clean low-end)
- **Snare slices:** Medium decay, slight reverb send
- **Hi-hat slices:** Short attack/decay, HP at 300 Hz

**Purpose:**
- Reconstruct classic boom bap patterns
- Mix individual elements (louder snare, tighter kick)
- Create bounce with kick/snare placement

**Typical Pattern:**
```
Kick on 1 and 3 (downbeats)
Snare on 2 and 4 (backbeat)
Hi-hats on offbeats (swing)
```

---

### Vinyl Sample Slicing

**Slice Division:** 1/4 or 1/8 (musical phrases)

**Why:**
- Preserve musical loops
- Chop hooks, chord stabs, horn hits
- Rearrange melodic elements

**Workflow:**
1. Find vinyl sample with musical content
2. Set BPM (original tempo)
3. Auto-Slice @ 1/4 or 1/8 (match musical phrases)
4. Trigger slices to create new melodies

**Common Sources:**
- Soul/funk loops
- Jazz piano stabs
- Horn section hits
- Basslines

---

### Chop and Swing Technique

**Key Setting:** Swing knob (60-70% for classic hip-hop feel)

**Why:**
- Rigid quantization = robotic
- Swing = human groove
- Classic MPC/SP-1200 feel

**Workflow:**
1. Slice break at 1/16
2. Quantize slices to grid
3. Add swing (65% for J Dilla feel)
4. Vary velocities (emphasize snare, soften hi-hats)

**Result:** Bouncy, head-nodding groove

---

### Velocity Layering for Dynamics

**Hip-Hop Velocity Map:**
- **Kick:** 90-100% (punch)
- **Snare:** 100% (emphasis)
- **Hi-hats (downbeat):** 75-85%
- **Hi-hats (offbeat):** 60-70% (ghost notes)

**Why:**
- Creates dynamics and movement
- Mimics live drumming
- Prevents robotic sound

---

## Trap/Modern Hip-Hop Slicing

### Core Philosophy
**Tight, quantized, aggressive - less swing, more precision**

---

### 808 Pattern Slicing

**Slice Division:** 1/8 or 1/4 (simpler than boom bap)

**Why:**
- Trap uses less complex drum patterns
- Focus on 808 slides and hi-hat rolls
- Tight, on-grid placement

**Workflow:**
1. Slice 808 hits and hi-hat samples
2. Keep kick/snare dry and tight
3. Hi-hat rolls at 1/32 (rapid fire)
4. 808 slides via pitch automation

---

### Hi-Hat Roll Slicing

**Slice Division:** 1/32 (rapid rolls)

**Configuration:**
- Very short decay (10-20 ms)
- High-pass at 500 Hz (bright)
- Velocity ramp (crescendo/decrescendo)

**Workflow:**
1. Slice single hi-hat hit
2. Program 1/32 note pattern (triplet or straight)
3. Velocity automation (builds tension)
4. Common before drops/transitions

---

### Vocal Chop Slicing

**Slice Division:** Manual (syllable-based)

**Why:**
- Vocal chops are melodic/rhythmic elements
- Each syllable = unique slice
- Creative rearrangement for hooks

**Workflow:**
1. Load vocal phrase
2. Manually slice at syllable boundaries
3. Map to keyboard (C5, D5, E5, etc.)
4. Play melodically or rhythmically

**Common Uses:**
- "Yeah" chops on downbeats
- "Huh" ad-libs between lyrics
- Melodic vocal stabs

---

## Drill Slicing Strategy

### Core Philosophy
**Dark, aggressive, sliding 808s with tight hi-hats**

---

### Slide 808 Slicing

**Slice Strategy:** Single 808 hit, pitch slides via automation

**Configuration:**
- Long decay (sustain for slides)
- Pitch envelope enabled
- Glide/portamento enabled

**Workflow:**
1. Slice single 808 hit
2. Trigger note
3. Second note = slide destination (glide enabled)
4. Creates signature drill bass slides

---

### UK Drill Hi-Hat Patterns

**Slice Division:** 1/16 and 1/32 (complex rolls)

**Pattern:**
- Fast rolls (1/32) between kicks
- Stutters and triplets
- Very tight, quantized

**Velocity:**
- Rolls start quiet, build to loud (tension)

---

## Drum & Bass / Breakbeat Slicing

### Core Philosophy
**Amen chops, rapid rearrangement, high-energy breaks**

---

### Amen Break Slicing

**Slice Division:** 1/16 or 1/32 (extreme granularity)

**Why:**
- Amen is THE breakbeat for DnB
- Needs fine slicing for complex edits
- Rearrange for 170+ BPM patterns

**Workflow:**
1. Load Amen Break (original @ ~138 BPM)
2. Time-stretch to 170-180 BPM
3. Auto-Slice @ 1/16 or 1/32
4. Rearrange: shuffle snares, double-time kicks, rapid hi-hats

**Key Technique:** "Reese bass" from pitched-down Amen snare

---

### Jungle Ragga Vocal Slicing

**Slice Division:** Manual (phrase-based)

**Why:**
- Ragga vocals = rhythmic MCing
- Slice at phrase boundaries
- Trigger in time with break

**Common Pattern:**
- Vocal stab on snare hits
- Call-and-response with break
- Reverb/delay throws

## Common Mistakes by Genre

### Boom Bap Mistakes

**Mistake 1: No Swing**
- Sounds robotic
- Lacks groove
- Fix: Add 60-70% swing

**Mistake 2: All Velocities Equal**
- No dynamics
- Lifeless
- Fix: Vary velocities (snare loud, hats quiet)

**Mistake 3: Over-Slicing**
- 1/32 divisions unnecessary
- Hard to rearrange
- Fix: Use 1/16 for breaks

---

### Trap Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Swing on Hi-Hats**
- Should be tight and quantized
- Fix: 0% swing, on-grid

**Mistake 2: Slow Hi-Hat Rolls**
- Not aggressive enough
- Fix: 1/32 divisions with velocity ramps

**Mistake 3: Weak 808 Slides**
- Not using glide/portamento
- Fix: Enable glide, automate pitch

---

## The Bottom Line

**Boom bap slicing uses 1/16 divisions with 60-70% swing and wide velocity variation (60-100%) for groove. Classic breaks (Amen, Think) are sliced fine, rearranged with kick on 1 & 3, snare on 2 & 4. Trap uses tighter quantization (0% swing), 1/32 hi-hat rolls with velocity ramps, and sliding 808s via pitch automation. Drill focuses on dark, aggressive slides and complex hi-hat patterns. DnB uses extreme slicing (1/32) of breaks at 170+ BPM. Genre determines slice division, swing, and velocity approach.**

**Golden Rules:**
- **Boom Bap:** Swing + velocity variation = groove
- **Trap:** Tight quantization + hi-hat rolls = aggression
- **Drill:** Sliding 808s + dark vibe = signature sound
- **DnB:** Fine slicing + time-stretch = energy
