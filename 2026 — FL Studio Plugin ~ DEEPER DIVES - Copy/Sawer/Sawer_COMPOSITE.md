# Sawer - 00 START HERE

## Purpose
Sawer is a modeled vintage subtractive synth inspired by the Soviet Polivoks. Its defining character is the aggressive, unstable filter and thick low-end.

## 10-Minute Orientation
1. Load a default patch and play low notes.
2. Sweep Cutoff and Resonance to hear the Polivoks growl.
3. Add Sub oscillator and adjust Phase for low-end weight.
4. Enable Unison and set light detune.
5. Route LFO -> Cutoff in the Mod Matrix for motion.

## 🎯 What is Sawer?

Sawer is a detailed emulation of the **Polivoks**, a legendary Soviet analog synthesizer from the 1980s. It is known for its gritty, aggressive, and unstable filter character. Unlike clean digital synths, Sawer aims for "imperfection" and warmth. It features a unique filter design, syncable sub-oscillator, and a powerful arpeggiator/effects section.

**Key Capabilities:**
- **Polivoks Filter:** 12dB/24dB LP/BP/HP with unique grit.
- **Oscillators:** Saw + Sub (with phase/detune) + Noise.
- **Sync:** Hard sync for tearing leads.
- **Unison:** 8-Voice unison for massive width.
- **Matrix:** Modulation matrix for LFO/Envelope routing.
- **Effects:** Chorus, Phaser, Delay, Reverb (Vintage style).

# 00: What Sawer Is and When to Use It

## Purpose and Identity
Sawer is a subtractive synthesizer that meticulously emulates the **Polivoks**, a Soviet-era analog synth. Its identity is defined by "imperfection"—it reproduces the unstable voltage behavior, the grit of the hardware oscillators, and most importantly, the unique, aggressive character of the Polivoks filter. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]

## Where it Fits in FL Studio
It is the "Analog Rebel" of the FL Studio lineup. Where plugins like Harmless or Sytrus provide clean, surgical digital synthesis, Sawer provides raw, harmonically rich, and slightly unpredictable analog tones.

## When to Use vs. When Not to Use
| Use Sawer When... | Avoid Sawer When... |
| :--- | :--- |
| You need "stinky" or "gritty" analog bass. | You need clean, pristine digital FM bells. |
| You want 80s synthwave or vintage lo-fi vibes. | You need hyper-modern, complex "Serum-style" wavetables. |
| You want a filter that screams when the resonance is high. | You need a subtle, transparent filter that doesn't color the sound. |
| You need a simple, fast UI for sound design. | You need deep, multi-operator FM routing. |

## 60-Second Mental Model: The "Soviet Grit"
Think of Sawer as a vintage engine that leaks a little oil. The Sawtooth wave isn't a perfect triangle; it has "jitter." The filter doesn't just cut frequencies; it adds saturation and bite. Every parameter is designed to add "warmth" (controlled distortion) to the signal.

## Hip-Hop/R&B Context
- **Trap Bass:** Use the Sub-oscillator for massive, slightly distorted low-end.
- **Moody R&B Pads:** Use the Unison and the built-in Chorus for that "drifting" 80s feel.
- **Lo-Fi Melodies:** The "unstable" nature of the oscillators makes it perfect for "dusty" keys.
- **West Coast Leads:** The Hard Sync is perfect for those high-pitched "whine" leads.

# 01: UI Map and Signal Flow

## UI Sections Tour
Sawer uses a classic one-page layout, making it one of the fastest synths to program in FL Studio. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]

### 1. Oscillator Section
- **Saw & Sub:** The core sound source.
- **Sub Phase:** Adjusts the alignment of the sub-oscillator (crucial for bass impact).
- **Noise:** Adds vintage floor noise or percussive texture.
- **Sync:** Enables hard sync between oscillators for "tearing" lead sounds.

### 2. Filter Section (The Polivoks Heart)
- **Mode:** LP (Low Pass), BP (Band Pass), HP (High Pass).
- **Slope:** 12dB (Classic) or 24dB (Steep).
- **Cutoff & Resonance:** The resonance here is "dirty" and adds saturation as it increases.
- **KBD (Keyboard Tracking):** Makes the filter open more as you play higher notes.

### 3. Envelopes (Amp & Filter)
- **Amp ADSR:** Standard volume control.
- **Filter ADSR:** Dedicated envelope for the filter cutoff movement.

### 4. LFO & Matrix
- **LFO:** Speed and Waveform for modulation.
- **Matrix:** 4-slot routing table to connect LFO/Env to parameters (Pitch, Filter, Pan, etc.).

### 5. Unison & Effects
- **Unison:** Up to 8 voices for massive stereo width.
- **FX:** Chorus, Phaser, Delay, and Reverb designed with a "vintage" algorithm.

## Signal Flow
`Oscillators (Saw + Sub + Noise) -> Filter -> Amp Envelope -> Unison Engine -> Effects Section -> Output`

## Things Beginners Misunderstand
- **The "Phase" Knob:** It doesn't just move the wave; it changes how the Sub sits with the Saw. If your bass sounds "thin," move this knob.
- **Resonance Gain:** On many synths, high resonance kills the bass. On Sawer, the Polivoks model tries to keep some grit even at high resonance.
- **Mono vs. Poly:** Sawer defaults to Poly. For aggressive bass or leads, switch to "Mono" in the top bar to get authentic synth behavior.

# 02: Core Techniques and Best Practices

## 1. The "Phase-Aligned" 808
When using Sawer for sub-bass, keep the **Sub Phase** consistent. If it's set to "Free," every note will have a different amount of punch. Set it to a fixed position (try 0% or 180%) for a consistent "thump" in every hit. [SRC: REPUTABLE-03]

## 2. The "Screaming" Sync Lead
Enable **Hard Sync**. Map the **LFO** to the **Oscillator Pitch** in the Matrix. This creates that classic "tearing" sound where the harmonics of the sync shift rhythmically.

## 3. Vintage "Drift" Pad
To get a truly "unstable" analog feel:
1. Enable **Unison** (4 voices).
2. Set **Unison Detune** to 10-15%.
3. In the Matrix, route a slow LFO to **Master Pitch** with a very small amount (1-2%). This simulates pitch drift.

## 4. "Dirty" Filter Plucks
Set the Filter to **LP 24dB**. Crank the **Resonance** to 70%. Use a fast **Filter Envelope** with 0 Attack and short Decay. The result is a "chirp" that sounds more like a hardware synth than a digital one.

## 5. Noise-Layered Percussion
Turn down the Saw and Sub oscillators. Turn up the **Noise**. Use a very short Amp Decay and Filter Decay. This creates "analog hats" or "snares" that have a unique Soviet texture.

## 6. Matrix "Vel-to-Filter"
In modern R&B, "feeling" is everything. Route **Velocity** to **Filter Cutoff** in the Matrix. Now, playing harder makes the sound brighter, just like a real acoustic instrument.

## 7. The "Warm" Button (Secret Trick)
Check the top bar/menu for "Warm" or "Analog" options (depending on version). Enabling this adds a subtle saturation to the master output that glues the sound together.

## Common Pitfalls + Fixes
- **Pitfall:** Sound is too "buzzy."
  - **Fix:** Lower the Oscillator mix or apply more Filter Damping/LP filter.
- **Pitfall:** Unison is making it muddy.
  - **Fix:** Use the **Unison Pan** to spread voices wider, or reduce voices to 2.
- **Pitfall:** Arpeggiator is out of time.
  - **Fix:** Ensure the "Sync" button in the Arp section is enabled.

# 03: Vibe Translation Guide

## Vibe Targets in Sawer

### 1. Moody (Dark, Nostalgic, Gritty)
- **The Lever:** **LP Filter + Noise + Sub Phase.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Close the LP filter (24dB mode) to around 300Hz.
  - Add 5% Noise for "tape hiss" texture.
  - Set Sub Phase to 180° for a slightly "hollow" but deep bass.
- **What to listen for:** A muffled, "dusty" tone that feels like an old recording from the 80s.
- **Pitfall:** Too much resonance will make it "whistle" and break the moody vibe. Keep resonance below 20%.

### 2. Upbeat (Aggressive, Tearing, Modern Trap)
- **The Lever:** **Hard Sync + High Resonance.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Enable Hard Sync.
  - Set Resonance to 60%+ for that "acid" bite.
  - Use a fast Filter Envelope to create "stabs."
- **What to listen for:** A sound that "cuts" through the mix and demands attention.
- **Pitfall:** If the decay is too long, the aggressive filter will become annoying. Keep stabs short.

### 3. Psychedelic (Trippy, Moving, Evolving)
- **The Lever:** **Matrix Modulation + Phaser.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Map LFO to Filter Cutoff and Phaser Frequency.
  - Use a slow Sine LFO for "swirling" motion.
  - Enable high Unison detune (20%).
- **What to listen for:** A sound that feels like it's "breathing" or "spinning" around the stereo field.
- **Pitfall:** Don't modulate the pitch too much or it will become unmusical/out of tune.

### 4. Jazzy (Warm, Sophisticated, Analog Keys)
- **The Lever:** **Soft Attack + Chorus + Velocity.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Use a 50ms Attack on the Amp Envelope for "soft" starts.
  - Set Unison to 2 voices for a subtle "stereo width."
  - Route Velocity to Filter in the Matrix.
- **What to listen for:** A "Rhodes-like" response where soft playing is dark and hard playing is bright.
- **Pitfall:** Avoid the "Noise" knob; keep the signal clean and "classy."

### 5. Vibey (Lush, Wide, "Ear-Candy")
- **The Lever:** **Unison + Delay + Retrig.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - 8 Voices of Unison with high Pan spread.
  - Use the built-in Delay with "Ping-Pong" enabled.
  - Set LFO to "Retrig" so the movement restarts on every note.
- **What to listen for:** A "expensive" sounding pad or lead that fills the speakers effortlessly.
- **Pitfall:** Ensure the low-end is mono; if the unison spreads the sub-bass too wide, the mix will collapse.

# Polivoks Character: The Soviet Soul

## What is a Polivoks?
The Polivoks was a Soviet-era synthesizer (1982–1990) designed to be the Russian answer to the Minimoog. Because of the shortage of standard Western components, the engineers used "unconventional" Soviet military-grade parts. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]

## The "Dirty" Filter
The most famous part of the Polivoks (and Sawer) is the filter. Most filters use "operational amplifiers," but the Polivoks used a simpler "programmable operational amplifier" (KR140UD12).
- **Result:** When you increase resonance, the filter becomes unstable and creates a unique "grit" and "growl" that sounds like distortion but is actually filter feedback.
- **In Sawer:** Switch between the 12dB and 24dB modes. The 24dB mode is where the "Polivoks Growl" is most apparent.

## Sub-Oscillator Phase
On the original hardware, the sub-oscillator was often slightly out of phase or "drifted" from the main oscillator. Sawer captures this with the **Sub Phase** knob.
- **Why it matters:** In modern Hip-Hop, "phase" determines how the synth interacts with the 808/Kick. Moving the phase can make your bass sound "in front" or "behind" the drum.

## Why use Sawer today?
In an era of perfect digital synths, Sawer provides "texture." It sounds like an old machine that might break at any moment. For genres like **Lo-Fi**, **Dark Trap**, and **Synthwave**, this instability is exactly what creates the "vibe."

| Mistake | Symptom | The Fix | How to Verify |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Too much Unison on Bass** | Bass sounds weak, phasing, or "hollow." | Reduce Unison to 1 voice or 0% detune. | Check the Master meter for low-end consistency. |
| **Resonance too high on sub** | "Whistling" sound masks the actual bass note. | Lower the `Resonance` or `Cutoff`. | You should hear the "thump" more than the "whistle." |
| **Free-running LFO** | Rhythmic modulation is different every time. | Enable `Retrig` on the LFO section. | Every note should have the exact same "wobble." |
| **Poly mode on Leads** | Notes overlap and sound messy. | Switch `Voices Mode` to `Mono` or `Legato`. | Only one note should play at a time. |

# Preset Taxonomy

## Bank Structure
Sawer's factory presets are categorized by their role in a vintage or electronic production. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]

### Categories
1. **Bass:** Aggressive, snappy, and deep. (Good for Upbeat/Moody)
2. **Lead:** Hard sync, singing, and tearing sounds. (Good for Upbeat/Psychedelic)
3. **Pads:** Lush, detuned, and evolving textures. (Good for Vibey/Moody)
4. **Arp:** Rhythmic sequences and noise-based hits. (Good for Upbeat)
5. **Keys:** Analog electric pianos and organ-like tones. (Good for Jazzy/Lo-Fi)

## Vibe Tagging Strategy
When organizing custom Sawer patches:
- `[VINT]` - For authentic 80s Polivoks sounds.
- `[GRIT]` - For highly resonant/distorted "stinky" patches.
- `[CLEAN]` - For jazzy/sophisticated keys.
- `[DRFT]` - For patches with significant pitch LFO/drift.

## Auditioning Tips
- **The "Res" Test:** Turn the Resonance up to 50%. If the sound starts to "scream" in a musical way, it's a good Polivoks-style patch.
- **The "Sub" Test:** Mute the main Saw wave. If the Sub-oscillator sounds solid and "round," the patch is well-built for modern bass.

# Genre Rules: Hip-Hop & R&B

## Low-End Consistency (The Phase Rule)
- **Rule:** For modern 808-style bass, keep the **Sub Phase** locked. "Free" phase creates inconsistent peaks that mess up your limiter's headroom.
- **Rule:** If the bass feels "weak" in the mix, flip the **Sub Phase** 180°. Phasing cancellation between the Saw and Sub is common.

## Analog "Warmth" (The Noise Rule)
- **Rule:** Don't be afraid of the **Noise** knob for lo-fi hip-hop. 3-5% Noise adds an authentic analog "floor" that glues the track together.
- **Rule:** For clean R&B, keep Noise at 0% to avoid "muddy" silence.

## Unison Strategy
- **Rule:** For leads, use 4-8 voices for "vibey" width.
- **Rule:** For main bass, use **1 voice (Mono)**. Wide bass in Sawer often leads to phase cancellation and "hollow" low-end. [SRC: REPUTABLE-03]

## Filter "Stink" (The Polivoks Rule)
- **Rule:** The Polivoks filter is aggressive. If the sound is too "harsh" for R&B, use the **12dB slope** instead of 24dB. It is smoother and more "musical."
- **Rule:** For "Stinky" trap bass, use **24dB LP** and turn the **Resonance** up to 30%. This adds the signature "Soviet growl."

## Mix Context
- **Rule:** Sawer is harmonically rich. Always apply a **Low-Cut** on lead patches at 300Hz to leave room for the kick and 808.
- **Rule:** If the synth sounds "too digital," add a subtle **Chorus** (Mix 20%). Analog synths are never perfectly "still."

- Harmonic "tearing" that sounds like a wah-wah pedal but more aggressive.

- **Sync Sweep:** Automate the Sync Frequency over a 4-bar phrase. This is the hallmark of the "West Coast Whine."

# Genre Style Board: Sawer

## Hip-Hop
- **Technique:** "Soviet Sub." Use the Sub oscillator with the 24dB LP filter. The Polivoks character adds a natural saturation that makes the bass audible on small speakers without needing extra distortion plugins.
- **Reference Idea:** Think of the gritty, analog basslines in classic Dr. Dre or modern Travis Scott tracks.

## Rap
- **Technique:** "Toxic Sync Lead." Use Hard Sync with a fast pitch LFO. It creates a "reedy," aggressive tone that sits perfectly over loud 808s.
- **Reference Idea:** High-pitched West Coast G-Funk style leads with a Soviet twist.

## R&B / Pop-Hip-Hop
- **Technique:** "Vibey Analog Clouds." 8-voice Unison with maximum detune and chorus. It creates a "wash" of sound that feels nostalgic and expensive.
- **Reference Idea:** The lush, detuned synth pads in modern PBR&B (PartyNextDoor, The Weeknd).

## Key Technique References
- **Analog Drift:** LFO (1%) -> Master Pitch. [SRC: HW-HIST]
- **Soviet Filter Growl:** Resonance > 30% on LP 24dB. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]
- **Sub-Bass Punch:** Fixed Sub Phase at 180°. [SRC: REPUTABLE-03]

Sawer’s 4-slot matrix is the key to breaking its static analog sound. It allows you to create "Responsive" instruments. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]

## Column A: The Source
- **LFO:** Constant rhythmic movement.
- **Env:** Movement that happens once per note.
- **Vel:** Reaction to how hard you play (The "Human" factor).
- **MW (Mod Wheel):** Real-time performance control.

## Column B: The Destination
- **Pitch:** Vibrato or Sync-Sweeps.
- **Filter:** Tone movement or "Wah" effects.
- **Volume:** Dynamics or Tremolo.
- **Pan:** Stereo movement.

## Professional Logic Recipes:

### 1. The "Humanized" Filter (Jazzy/Vibey)
- **Source:** Velocity (Vel)
- **Destination:** Filter Cutoff
- **Amount:** +30%
- **Why:** In real instruments, harder hits are brighter. This makes Sawer feel less like a "computer."

### 2. The "Analog Drift" (Moody/Retro)
- **Source:** LFO
- **Destination:** Pitch
- **Amount:** 1% - 2% (Very subtle)
- **LFO Speed:** 1.5Hz (Slow)
- **Why:** Mimics the unstable voltage of Soviet hardware.

### 3. The "Sync Screamer" (Upbeat/Rap)
- **Source:** Filter Envelope (Env)
- **Destination:** Pitch (Sync Frequency)
- **Amount:** +60%
- **Requirement:** Hard Sync must be ON.
- **Why:** Creates a "tearing" harmonic sweep at the start of every lead note.

### 4. The "Rhythmic Panner" (Psychedelic)
- **Source:** LFO (Sine)
- **Destination:** Pan
- **Amount:** 100%
- **LFO Speed:** 1/4 or 1/8 note.
- **Why:** Makes the sound dance around the listener's head.

# Polivoks Op-Amp Emulation: The Dirty Secret

## The KR140UD12 Chip
Standard filters (Moog, Roland) use high-quality operational amplifiers. The Soviet Polivoks used the **KR140UD12**, which was electrically "noisy" and had a slow slew rate. [SRC: HW-HIST]

## Technical Consequences in Sawer:
1. **Self-Oscillation Distortion:** When resonance is high, the filter starts to "scream" (produce its own frequency). In Sawer, this oscillation is deliberately "impure," adding a layer of saturation that sounds like a tube amp.
2. **Phase Instability:** The oscillators in Sawer don't start at the exact same phase every time. This creates a "random" punch in the low-end.
   - **Research Tip:** If your 808 layer sounds different every time you play it, it’s the Sawer phase instability. Switch the **Sub Phase** to a fixed position to override this.

## How to use "The Grit" in Mixes:
- **Low Pass 24dB:** This is where the emulation is most aggressive. Use it for **Trap Bass** to add mid-range harmonics that help the bass cut through iPhone speakers.
- **Warm Mode:** This simulates the Master Output saturation of the original unit. Turn it on to "glue" the oscillators together before they hit the mixer.
