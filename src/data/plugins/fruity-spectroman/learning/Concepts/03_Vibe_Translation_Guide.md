# Vibe Translation Guide - Fruity Spectroman

## Introduction

While Fruity Spectroman is a technical analysis tool, understanding how different **vibes and emotional qualities** translate to **spectral signatures** helps you achieve specific sonic aesthetics intentionally. This guide connects the feeling you want with what you should see in the spectrum analyzer.

---

## Vibe-to-Spectrum Translation

### 🌙 **MOODY** Spectrum Profile

#### Sonic Characteristics
- Dark, brooding, introspective
- Subdued top-end, emphasized low-mids
- Weight and depth without brightness
- Intimate, close, heavy atmosphere

#### What the Spectrum Should Show

**Frequency Distribution:**
```
20-60 Hz:    Strong presence (deep foundation)
60-200 Hz:   Emphasized (warmth and weight)
200-500 Hz:  Moderate to strong (body and darkness)
500-2k Hz:   Controlled (not overly present)
2k-5k Hz:    Restrained (minimal brightness)
5k-10k Hz:   Reduced (subtle detail only)
10k-20k Hz:  Minimal (dark, closed-in feel)
```

**Visual Profile:**
- **Low-end dominant:** Red/yellow in bass and low-mids
- **Mid-range moderate:** Green/yellow in mids
- **High-end subdued:** Blue/cyan in highs, fading to black at top

**Vectorscope:**
- Narrow to moderate width
- Centered bass and kick
- Intimate stereo field (not overly wide)

#### Mixing Considerations for Moody Vibe
- **Boost:** 60-200 Hz for weight, 200-400 Hz for warmth
- **Cut:** 5-10 kHz for reduced brightness, 10+ kHz for darkness
- **Avoid:** Excessive air (above 12 kHz), harsh 3-5 kHz
- **Reference spectrum:** Should feel "bottom-heavy" visually

**Example Genres:**
- Lo-fi hip-hop, dark trap, ambient, downtempo

---

### ☀️ **UPBEAT** Spectrum Profile

#### Sonic Characteristics
- Bright, energetic, forward-moving
- Clear top-end, punchy low-end
- Open, exciting, dynamic
- Presence and clarity across the spectrum

#### What the Spectrum Should Show

**Frequency Distribution:**
```
20-60 Hz:    Solid but controlled (impact without mud)
60-200 Hz:   Present (groove and movement)
200-500 Hz:  Moderate (clean, not muddy)
500-2k Hz:   Strong (energy and forward momentum)
2k-5k Hz:    Prominent (clarity and presence)
5k-10k Hz:   Strong (brightness and excitement)
10k-20k Hz:  Present (air and sparkle)
```

**Visual Profile:**
- **Balanced distribution:** Energy across full spectrum
- **Extended highs:** Red/yellow extending into high frequencies
- **Clean low-mids:** Less buildup in 200-500 Hz mud zone
- **Bright presence:** Strong showing at 2-5 kHz and above

**Vectorscope:**
- Wider stereo field
- Dynamic movement (more variation)
- Clear separation between centered and wide elements

#### Mixing Considerations for Upbeat Vibe
- **Boost:** 2-5 kHz for presence, 8-12 kHz for air and sparkle
- **Cut:** 200-400 Hz to reduce mud, sub-30 Hz rumble
- **Enhance:** Stereo width on supporting elements
- **Reference spectrum:** Should feel "lifted" and extended

**Example Genres:**
- Pop, house, funk, disco, dance

---

### 🌀 **PSYCHEDELIC** Spectrum Profile

#### Sonic Characteristics
- Swirling, expansive, textured
- Rich harmonics and modulation
- Wide stereo field with movement
- Complex frequency content with evolving character

#### What the Spectrum Should Show

**Frequency Distribution:**
```
20-60 Hz:    Variable (pulsing, modulated)
60-200 Hz:   Dynamic (breathing, moving)
200-500 Hz:  Rich (warmth and body)
500-2k Hz:   Full (harmonic complexity)
2k-5k Hz:    Detailed (textural elements)
5k-10k Hz:   Extended (shimmer and space)
10k-20k Hz:  Pronounced (reverb tails, high harmonics)
```

**Visual Profile:**
- **Dynamic movement:** Spectrum constantly shifting
- **Harmonic richness:** Multiple simultaneous peaks
- **Extended range:** Content visible across full 20-20k range
- **Modulation visible:** Pulsing or sweeping patterns

**Vectorscope:**
- Wide, active stereo field
- Circular/oval patterns (broad width)
- Movement and variation (not static)
- Possible phase effects (controlled)

#### Mixing Considerations for Psychedelic Vibe
- **Emphasize:** Stereo width, reverb depth, harmonic saturation
- **Modulate:** Filters, panning, delay times (creates spectral movement)
- **Layer:** Multiple elements in overlapping frequency zones
- **Texture:** Chorus, flanger, phaser adds spectral complexity
- **Reference spectrum:** Should appear "alive" with movement

**Example Genres:**
- Psytrance, psychedelic rock, experimental electronic, dub

---

### 🎷 **JAZZY** Spectrum Profile

#### Sonic Characteristics
- Warm, natural, dynamic
- Emphasis on mid-range presence
- Organic top-end (not hyped)
- Room for tonal complexity

#### What the Spectrum Should Show

**Frequency Distribution:**
```
20-60 Hz:    Subtle (natural bass, not boosted)
60-200 Hz:   Warm and present (acoustic bass fundamental)
200-500 Hz:  Strong (warmth, body, richness)
500-2k Hz:   Prominent (instrumental presence)
2k-5k Hz:    Natural (clarity without harshness)
5k-10k Hz:   Smooth (cymbals, brass overtones)
10k-20k Hz:  Gentle roll-off (natural, not hyped)
```

**Visual Profile:**
- **Mid-range focused:** Red/yellow centered around 200 Hz - 5 kHz
- **Natural roll-off:** Gradual fade in extreme lows and highs
- **Balanced warmth:** Emphasis on low-mids and mids
- **Not overly processed:** Smooth, organic spectral shape

**Vectorscope:**
- Moderate width (natural stereo)
- Centered bass and kick
- Width from room ambience and instruments
- Realistic stereo imaging

#### Mixing Considerations for Jazzy Vibe
- **Preserve:** Natural dynamics (light compression)
- **Enhance:** 200-800 Hz for warmth, 2-4 kHz for presence
- **Avoid:** Over-EQ'd, hyped sound; excessive sub-bass
- **Room tone:** Reverb adds spectral complexity in 1-10 kHz
- **Reference spectrum:** Should look "musical" not "produced"

**Example Genres:**
- Jazz (obviously), neo-soul, lo-fi, acoustic

---

### 🌊 **VIBEY** Spectrum Profile

#### Sonic Characteristics
- Lush, textured, immersive
- Rich mid-range and controlled lows
- Spacious but intimate
- Emotional depth and atmosphere

#### What the Spectrum Should Show

**Frequency Distribution:**
```
20-60 Hz:    Controlled (foundation without boom)
60-200 Hz:   Warm and full (richness)
200-500 Hz:  Present (body and emotion)
500-2k Hz:   Strong (musical core)
2k-5k Hz:    Smooth (clarity without edge)
5k-10k Hz:   Spacious (reverb, delay, air)
10k-20k Hz:  Delicate (subtle sparkle)
```

**Visual Profile:**
- **Smooth distribution:** No harsh peaks or valleys
- **Lush mid-range:** Yellow/green showing body and warmth
- **Controlled extremes:** Neither boomy bass nor piercing highs
- **Reverb presence:** Extended high-frequency content from space

**Vectorscope:**
- Moderate to wide stereo
- Smooth, circular patterns
- Depth from reverb and delay
- Not aggressively widened

#### Mixing Considerations for Vibey Vibe
- **Layer:** Pads, textures, atmospheric elements
- **Space:** Reverb extends spectrum into 5-15 kHz range
- **Saturation:** Adds harmonic richness (visible as added overtones)
- **EQ gently:** Smooth boosts/cuts, no harsh surgical moves
- **Reference spectrum:** Should feel "rich" and "full" across range

**Example Genres:**
- R&B, alternative R&B, chill-hop, indie electronic

## Vibe-Specific Spectrum Checks

### Quick Visual Vibe Verification

#### **Moody Check:**
- ✅ Strong low-end (red at 60-200 Hz)
- ✅ Dark top-end (blue/black above 8 kHz)
- ❌ Too much brightness in highs
- ❌ Thin in low-mids

#### **Upbeat Check:**
- ✅ Extended highs (visible content to 15+ kHz)
- ✅ Clean low-mids (not too much 200-500 Hz)
- ❌ Dark or dull top-end
- ❌ Muddy low-mids

#### **Psychedelic Check:**
- ✅ Moving, dynamic spectrum
- ✅ Wide stereo field (vectorscope oval)
- ✅ Harmonic complexity (multiple peaks)
- ❌ Static, unchanging spectrum
- ❌ Narrow stereo field

#### **Jazzy Check:**
- ✅ Mid-range prominence (500-5 kHz)
- ✅ Natural roll-off (no harsh edges)
- ✅ Moderate stereo width
- ❌ Hyper-compressed dynamics
- ❌ Artificial sub-bass boost

#### **Vibey Check:**
- ✅ Smooth, lush distribution
- ✅ Reverb presence (5-12 kHz)
- ✅ Controlled but full bass
- ❌ Harsh peaks anywhere
- ❌ Thin or hollow sound

## Spectral Adjustments for Vibe Shifts

### Transforming Vibe Through EQ

#### **To Make More Moody:**
- Cut 5-10 kHz (-2 to -4 dB) → reduces brightness
- Boost 200-400 Hz (+1 to +3 dB) → adds weight
- Roll off above 12 kHz → darkens top
- Check spectrum: Should become "bottom-heavy"

#### **To Make More Upbeat:**
- Boost 2-5 kHz (+2 to +4 dB) → adds presence
- Boost 10-15 kHz (+1 to +3 dB) → adds air
- Cut 200-400 Hz (-1 to -2 dB) → cleans up
- Check spectrum: Should extend higher with more energy

#### **To Make More Psychedelic:**
- Add modulation effects (spectrum will move)
- Widen stereo (vectorscope expands)
- Layer harmonics (add overtones)
- Add reverb (extends high-frequency tail)

#### **To Make More Jazzy:**
- Reduce extreme processing (spectrum smooths)
- Boost 500-1k Hz (+2 dB) → warmth
- Gentle high-shelf cut → natural roll-off
- Light compression (preserve dynamic spectrum)

#### **To Make More Vibey:**
- Add reverb/delay (check 5-12 kHz presence)
- Smooth EQ moves (no sharp peaks)
- Saturation adds harmonics (check overtone spread)
- Layer textures (fuller spectrum)

## The Bottom Line

**Spectroman helps you verify your vibe is translating as intended.** Each emotional quality has a corresponding spectral signature - moody mixes are dark and bottom-heavy, upbeat mixes are bright and extended, psychedelic mixes show movement and complexity, jazzy mixes are mid-focused and natural, vibey mixes are smooth and lush. Use these visual references to confirm your mixing decisions are supporting the intended emotional impact, not fighting against it.

**Remember:** The vibe comes from your musical choices first - Spectroman just confirms those choices are working spectrally.
