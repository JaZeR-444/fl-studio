```markdown
## Purpose
Spreader widens stereo images while preserving mono compatibility. It uses mid/side-style processing and a low-frequency bypass to keep bass centered.

## 10-Minute Orientation
1. Insert Spreader on a mono source.
2. Increase Spread to hear width.
3. Set Low Bypass to keep bass mono.
4. Use the vectorscope and phase meters to monitor stability.
```

```markdown
## 🎯 What is Spreader?

Spreader is a modern, high-fidelity stereo widening plugin designed to expand the spatial image of any audio source while maintaining strict **mono compatibility**. It is particularly powerful for mono inputs, synthesizing a wide stereo field without the common phase cancellation artifacts of traditional methods. It features a built-in vectorscope for visual feedback and a specialized "Low Bypass" section to keep bass frequencies centered and punchy.

**Key Capabilities:**
- **Advanced Widening:** Expands the stereo image up to 100%.
- **Guaranteed Mono Compatibility:** Prevents sound from vanishing on mono systems.
- **Low Bypass:** Excludes bass frequencies from the widening effect (10Hz - 10kHz).
- **Separation Control:** Balances the blend between mono center and stereo edges.
- **Vectorscope Visualization:** Real-time feedback of stereo width and balance.
- **Mono Switch:** Instantly collapses the low-end bypass signal to mono.
```

```markdown
# 00: What Spreader Is and When to Use It

## Purpose and Identity
Spreader is a modern, high-fidelity stereo widening tool. Its core identity is **Width without Compromise**. It uses advanced psychoacoustic algorithms to transform mono or narrow signals into massive stereo images while ensuring that the signal sounds identical when summed back to mono (No phase cancellation). [SRC: IL-MAN-07]

## Where it Fits in FL Studio
It is the "Professional" successor to the legacy Stereo Enhancer. It is best used during the mixing stage to give instruments their own space in the stereo field without clashing with the vocals or bass.

## When to Use vs. When Not to Use
| Use Spreader When... | Avoid Spreader When... |
| :--- | :--- |
| You want to make a mono vocal feel "wide." | You want to fix a lopsided recording (use Stereo Shaper). |
| You need to widen a synth pad while keeping the sub-bass mono. | You want to create a "rotating" leslie speaker effect. |
| You want visual feedback of your stereo image. | You need to narrow a sound (use the Pan knob). |
| You need a "lush" R&B feel on a mono piano. | You are working on a purely mono track. |

## 60-Second Mental Model: The "Expansion Fan"
Think of your sound as a folded paper fan.
1. **The Fold (Mono):** The sound is a single line in the middle.
2. **The Open (Width):** Spreader unfolds the fan to fill the sides.
3. **The Handle (Low Bypass):** You keep the "handle" of the fan (the bass) closed and centered, so the fan doesn't wobble.

## Hip-Hop/R&B Context
- **R&B Vocals:** Add subtle width (30-50%) to lead vocals to make them feel "expensive."
- **Trap 808s:** Use the **Low Bypass** to keep the sub frequencies mono while making the distorted high-mids of the 808 sound wide and aggressive.
- **Lo-Fi Rhodes:** Turn a dry, mono electric piano into a wide, "vibey" atmosphere.
- **Percussion:** Widen hi-hat rolls or percussive loops to make the drum pattern feel "immersive."
```

```markdown
# 01: UI Map and Signal Flow

## UI Sections Tour
Spreader features a clean, single-panel modern interface designed for speed and visual accuracy. [SRC: IL-MAN-07]

### 1. Visualization (The Vectorscope)
- **The Display:** Shows the stereo image in real-time. 
- **The Line:** A vertical line indicates a pure mono signal.
- **The Flower/Cloud:** Indicates stereo width. The wider the shape, the more stereo information.

### 2. Main Processing (Width & Separation)
- **Width:** Expands the signal into the stereo sides.
- **Separated/Merged:** Controls the balance between the center signal and the side signal. "Separated" pushes sound to the edges; "Merged" pulls it toward the middle.

### 3. Low Bypass Section (The Low-End Guard)
- **Freq Slider:** Selects the frequency below which the signal is NOT widened.
- **Mono Toggle:** Collapses the bypassed low frequencies into pure mono (essential for bass).

### 4. Gain & Master
- **Gain:** Adjusts the output level (useful as widening can sometimes change perceived loudness).

## Signal Flow
`Input Signal -> Frequency Splitter (Low/High) -> Low Path (Mono Bypass) -> High Path (Stereo Synthesis) -> Summing Engine -> Master Gain -> Output`

## Things Beginners Misunderstand
- **Mono Compatibility:** Many "wideners" create phase issues. Spreader is specifically designed to avoid this. If you turn FL's Master to mono, the Spreader effect disappears, but the *sound* doesn't get thin or quiet.
- **Separation vs. Width:** **Width** creates the stereo signal. **Separation** determines how much of that signal is heard relative to the original mono center.
- **Low Bypass:** This doesn't cut the bass; it just tells Spreader "don't widen these frequencies."
    
```

```markdown
# 02: Core Techniques and Best Practices

## 1. The "Laser-Focused" 808
If your 808 is wide and messy in the low-end:
1. Put Spreader on the 808 mixer track.
2. Set **Low Bypass** to 150Hz.
3. Enable the **Mono** switch in the bypass section.
4. Set **Width** to 60%.
- *Result:* Your sub-bass is perfectly mono and punchy, but the distortion/mids are wide and "mean."

## 2. Realistic "Vocal Doubling"
To make a lead vocal sound like it was recorded twice:
1. Use a mono vocal track.
2. Set **Width** to 40%.
3. Adjust **Separation** until the center of the vocal still feels strong but has a "halo" of width around it.

## 3. Cleaning Up "Lopsided" Samples
If a sample sounds "louder on the left":
1. Look at the **Vectorscope**.
2. Use the **Separation** slider to pull the signal back toward the center ("Merged").
3. Use the **Width** slider to re-expand it evenly.

## 4. The "Infinite" Pad Swell
Automate the **Width** parameter from 0% to 100% over 4 bars during a transition. Because Spreader is phase-safe, the swell will sound smooth and natural, unlike traditional stereo enhancers which might "wobble" the pitch.

## 5. Drum Bus Immersion
Instead of widening every drum individually:
1. Route all drums (except Kick) to a "Drum Bus."
2. Add Spreader with a low **Width** (20-30%).
- *Result:* The drums feel like they are "living in a space" rather than just being panned left or right.

## 6. High-Frequency "Sparkle"
Set the **Low Bypass** very high (e.g., 5kHz). Now, only the top-end air and transients are widened. This is a great trick for acoustic guitars or hi-hats to add "shimmer" without affecting the core tone.

## Common Pitfalls + Fixes
- **Pitfall:** Bass sounds "hollow."
  - **Fix:** Increase the **Low Bypass** frequency and ensure the **Mono** switch is engaged.
- **Pitfall:** Signal sounds too quiet after widening.
  - **Fix:** Increase the **Gain** knob inside Spreader.
- **Pitfall:** Vectorscope is just a vertical line.
  - **Fix:** You haven't increased the **Width** slider yet!
```

```markdown
# 03: Vibe Translation Guide

## Vibe Targets in Spreader

### 1. Moody (Dark, Intimate, Deep)
- **The Lever:** **Low Width + High Low-Bypass.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Keep **Width** at a modest 25-35%.
  - Set **Low Bypass** to 400Hz. This keeps the "warmth" in mono while the "dark" textures spread out.
- **What to listen for:** A sound that feels "deep" and "centered" but has a subtle atmosphere around it.
- **Pitfall:** Too much width makes moody sounds feel "plastic" or "synthetic." Keep it subtle.

### 2. Upbeat (High-Energy, Huge, Modern)
- **The Lever:** **Max Width + Mono Bypass.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Turn **Width** to 80-100%.
  - Set **Low Bypass** to 100Hz and enable the **Mono** switch.
  - Use on percussive loops or synth leads.
- **What to listen for:** An "explosive" stereo image that fills the speakers and creates energy.
- **Pitfall:** Ensure the snare doesn't lose its "center." If it does, reduce the **Separation**.

### 3. Psychedelic (Trippy, Moving, Fluid)
- **The Lever:** **Automated Width.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Right-click the **Width** knob and "Create Automation Clip."
  - Draw a rhythmic "pulse" shape (triangles or sine waves).
  - Add an external **Delay** or **Phaser** *before* Spreader.
- **What to listen for:** A sound that seems to "breathe" in and out of the speakers rhythmically.
- **Pitfall:** Don't automate too fast (no "vibrato" speeds); slow, fluid movements work best for psychedelic vibes.

### 4. Jazzy (Organic, Wide, Sophisticated)
- **The Lever:** **Separation + High Cut (External).**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Use Spreader on a mono piano or sax.
  - Adjust **Separation** to the "Merged" side to keep the instrument's "wood" in the center.
  - Use a moderate **Width** (40%).
- **What to listen for:** A "lush" and "expensive" sound that mimics a stereo microphone recording.
- **Pitfall:** Avoid 100% width on organic instruments; it can make them sound "phasey" and fake.

### 5. Vibey (Smooth, "Ear-Candy", Modern R&B)
- **The Lever:** **The "Flower" Vectorscope Shape.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Aim for a "round" flower shape on the vectorscope.
  - Use on background vocal harmonies or "glitter" synths.
  - Set **Width** to 60% and **Low Bypass** to 300Hz.
- **What to listen for:** A "smooth" and "silky" texture that floats perfectly around the lead vocal.
- **Pitfall:** If the "flower" is leaning to one side, use the mixer pan to balance it before it hits Spreader.
```

```markdown
# Vectorscope Interpretation Guide

## What is a Vectorscope?
The vectorscope in Spreader is a visual representation of the stereo field. It maps the relationship between the Left and Right channels. [SRC: IL-MAN-07]

## How to Read the Shapes:

### 1. The Vertical Line (|)
- **Meaning:** Your signal is 100% Mono.
- **Vibe:** Strong, centered, and punchy.
- **Action:** If you want width, start turning up the **Width** slider.

### 2. The Narrow Leaf (/\)
- **Meaning:** Subtle stereo width.
- **Vibe:** Natural and realistic.
- **Action:** Good for lead vocals or main melodic instruments.

### 3. The Wide Flower (O)
- **Meaning:** Massive stereo expansion.
- **Vibe:** Immersive and "vibey."
- **Action:** Perfect for pads, background vocals, and transition FX.

### 4. The Horizontal Blur (-)
- **Meaning:** Extreme out-of-phase signal.
- **Vibe:** Potentially "broken" or "washy."
- **Action:** Sum to mono to ensure the sound doesn't disappear. If it does, reduce **Width**.

### 5. The Lopsided Cloud (\ or /)
- **Meaning:** Your signal is panned more to one side.
- **Vibe:** Unbalanced and distracting.
- **Action:** Use the Mixer Pan knob *before* the Spreader to center the signal.

## Why it's useful for Hip-Hop:
- **Checking the 808:** If you see a "cloud" in the low frequencies (below the Low Bypass frequency), your bass is not mono and will cause problems in a club. 
- **Checking the Vocals:** Ensure the lead vocal stays as a "strong core" (narrow leaf) while the ad-libs are a "wide flower."
```

```markdown
# Genre Rules: Hip-Hop & R&B

## The 808 "Sub-Safe" Rule
- **Rule:** Never use a stereo widener on a main 808 without a low-end bypass.
- **Action:** In Spreader, set the **Low Bypass** to at least **120Hz** and enable the **Mono** switch. This ensures your 808 hits hard in mono club systems while still sounding wide on headphones.

## The Lead Vocal "Halo"
- **Rule:** For professional R&B vocals, don't go over **40% Width**.
- **Action:** Adjust the **Separation** toward "Merged" to keep the vocal's core "weight" in the center. This creates a "halo" of width rather than a "doubled" effect.

## Hi-Hat "Shimmer"
- **Rule:** Avoid widening low-frequency percussion (Kicks/Claps). Widen the high-frequency elements (Hats/Percs).
- **Action:** Set **Low Bypass** to **2kHz**. Now only the "air" of the hats is spread, creating a sophisticated top-end.

## Mono Summing Verification
- **Rule:** Always check your mix in **Mono** after using Spreader.
- **Action:** Use the FL Studio Master Mono switch. If the instrument's tone changes drastically, reduce the **Width** or adjust the **Separation**.

## Ad-Lib Immersion
- **Rule:** Ad-libs should always be wider than the lead vocal.
- **Action:** Use **80-100% Width** on ad-lib tracks. Since they are background elements, you can be more aggressive with the separation.
```

```markdown
# Genre Style Board: Spreader

## Hip-Hop
- **Technique:** "Mono-Safe 808." Use the Low Bypass at 150Hz. Widen the higher harmonics of the 808 to 100% width. This makes the bass feel like it's "wrapping around" the listener while keeping the sub-thump solid.
- **Reference Idea:** Think of the massive, distorted 808s in modern Travis Scott or Metro Boomin tracks that sound huge on headphones but don't lose power in a club.

## Rap
- **Technique:** "Explosive Ad-libs." Turn Width to 100% and Separation to "Separated." This pushes ad-libs to the far edges of the stereo field, leaving the entire center open for the main rap vocal.
- **Reference Idea:** The "surround sound" ad-libs found in modern trap (Migos, Gunna).

## R&B / Pop-Hip-Hop
- **Technique:** "Vocal Halo." Use 30-40% Width on the lead vocal. Adjust separation toward "Merged." This gives the vocal a professional "shimmer" and "space" without making it sound doubled or fake.
- **Reference Idea:** The expensive, wide vocal production in modern R&B like Summer Walker or Bryson Tiller.
```

```markdown
# Psychoacoustic Stereo Theory: Why Spreader is Safe

Traditional stereo widening usually relies on the **Haas Effect** (Delaying one side by 5-30ms) or **Phase Inversion**. Both of these cause massive problems when summed to mono (Phasing/Comb filtering). [SRC: REPUTABLE-08]

## Spreader’s Advanced Approach:

### 1. Complementary Comb Filtering
- Instead of just delaying a signal, Spreader uses a bank of filters that "sculpt" the L/R signals so that where one has a peak, the other has a dip. 
- **The Research Result:** When these signals are combined (Mono), the peaks and dips perfectly fill each other, returning the signal to its original state.

### 2. Frequency-Dependent Widening
- Spreader recognizes that our ears cannot perceive direction well in low frequencies.
- **The Lever:** The **Low Bypass** slider prevents the plugin from wasting processing power (and causing phase blur) on frequencies below 150Hz.

### 3. Localization Consistency
- Unlike a Chorus effect, Spreader doesn't "wobble" the pitch. It keeps the **Virtual Center** stable.
- **Production Value:** This is why it’s the best choice for Lead Vocals. You get the width without the singer sounding like they are "moving" or "pitchy."
```

```markdown
# Spreader vs. Stereo Enhancer: The Spatial Evolution

## Stereo Enhancer (The Legacy Tool)
- **Method:** Phase shifting and L/R delay (Hass Effect).
- **Pros:** Low CPU, simple.
- **Cons:** **Extreme Phase Issues.** If you sum to mono, the sound can drastically change or disappear completely.
- **The "Vibe":** Simple, but dangerous for professional mixing.

## Spreader (The Modern Pro)
- **Method:** Multi-band psychoacoustic spatial synthesis.
- **Pros:** **100% Mono Compatible.** The sound never disappears in mono. Includes Low Bypass and Vectorscope.
- **Cons:** Slightly higher CPU (still very low).
- **The "Vibe":** Professional, safe, and modern.

## Why Switch to Spreader?
1. **Club Compatibility:** Since Spreader doesn't mess with phase in the same way, your tracks won't lose their "soul" when played on mono club systems.
2. **Low-End Control:** You can widen a sound without the bass becoming "floaty" or weak.
3. **Visual Proof:** The vectorscope lets you *see* the width, which is essential for accurate mixing. [SRC: IL-MAN-07]
```
