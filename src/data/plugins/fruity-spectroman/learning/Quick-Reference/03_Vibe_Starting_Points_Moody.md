# Vibe Starting Points - MOODY

## Moody Spectrum Signature

**Target Visual Profile:**
- Dark, bottom-heavy spectrum
- Strong presence in bass and low-mids
- Subdued highs and top-end
- Intimate, closed-in visual appearance

---

## What to Look For in Spectroman

### Ideal Moody Spectrum

```
20-40 Hz:    Blue/minimal (clean foundation)
40-80 Hz:    Yellow/red (deep bass presence)
80-200 Hz:   Red/yellow (weight and warmth)
200-500 Hz:  Yellow/green (body and darkness)
500-2k Hz:   Green/yellow (controlled presence)
2k-5k Hz:    Green/cyan (restrained clarity)
5k-10k Hz:   Cyan/blue (reduced brightness)
10k-20k Hz:  Blue/black (dark top-end)
```

**Visual Characteristics:**
- Bottom-heavy: More energy in lower half of spectrum
- Warm tilt: Emphasis on 80-500 Hz
- Dark roll-off: Rapid fade above 5 kHz
- Not sparkly: Minimal content above 10 kHz

---

## Spectroman Checks for Moody Vibe

### ✅ Moody Vibe Confirmed When:

1. **Low-End Dominance**
   - Heavy red/yellow at 60-200 Hz
   - Visibly "bottom-heavy" spectrum
   - Strong foundation without boom

2. **Subdued Highs**
   - Blue/cyan above 5 kHz
   - Minimal content above 12 kHz
   - No harsh brightness

3. **Warm Low-Mids**
   - Present at 200-500 Hz (warmth zone)
   - Not excessive (avoid mud)
   - Adds emotional weight

4. **Controlled Presence Zone**
   - 2-5 kHz present but not aggressive
   - Green/cyan, not red (not in-your-face)
   - Smooth, not harsh

5. **Intimate Stereo Field**
   - Vectorscope shows moderate width
   - Not overly wide (keeps intimacy)
   - Centered low-end (vertical vector)

---

## EQ Adjustments for Moody Spectrum

### To Achieve Moody Visual Profile

**Boost Low-End Weight:**
- +2 to +4 dB at 80-120 Hz (warmth and body)
- +1 to +2 dB at 200-300 Hz (thickness)
- Check Spectroman: Should see increased red in low zone

**Reduce Brightness:**
- -2 to -4 dB at 5-8 kHz (reduce sparkle)
- High-shelf cut above 10 kHz (-2 to -3 dB)
- Check Spectroman: Highs should fade to blue/black

**Control Harshness:**
- -1 to -2 dB at 3-4 kHz (soften presence)
- Smooth rather than cut completely
- Check Spectroman: Less red in upper-mids

**Clean Sub-Bass:**
- High-pass at 30-40 Hz (remove rumble)
- Check Spectroman: No red below 30 Hz

## Moody Reference Spectrum Examples

### What Professional Moody Tracks Look Like

**Hip-Hop/Trap Moody:**
- Heavy 40-80 Hz (808s, sub-bass)
- Controlled 200-400 Hz (clean but warm)
- Present 2-3 kHz (vocal clarity)
- Dark above 8 kHz

**Lo-Fi/Chill Moody:**
- Rolled-off lows (less sub-bass than trap)
- Strong 200-500 Hz (warm, vintage vibe)
- Soft 2-5 kHz (gentle, not bright)
- Very dark above 10 kHz (dusty, lo-fi character)

**Alternative R&B Moody:**
- Moderate 40-100 Hz (controlled bass)
- Warm 200-400 Hz (emotional weight)
- Smooth 2-4 kHz (vocal intimacy)
- Gentle roll-off above 6 kHz

---

## Stereo Field (Vectorscope) for Moody

**Ideal Vectorscope Pattern:**
- Moderate width (not overly wide)
- Vertical component strong (mono bass/kick)
- Circular/oval, not horizontal
- Intimate, not expansive

**Moody Stereo Settings:**
- Keep bass centered (check vectorscope is vertical in low-end)
- Moderate width on pads/synths
- Avoid extreme stereo enhancement
- Use mono reverb or narrow reverb width

## Common Moody Spectrum Mistakes

### ❌ **Too Much Sub-Bass**
**Visual:** Heavy red below 40 Hz  
**Issue:** Loses headroom, doesn't translate  
**Fix:** High-pass at 35-40 Hz

### ❌ **Too Bright**
**Visual:** Red/yellow extending to 12+ kHz  
**Issue:** Loses dark, moody character  
**Fix:** Reduce high-shelf, cut 5-8 kHz

### ❌ **Muddy**
**Visual:** Excessive red at 200-500 Hz  
**Issue:** Warm becomes muddy and unclear  
**Fix:** Reduce 300-400 Hz, maintain 2-3 kHz clarity

### ❌ **Lacks Clarity**
**Visual:** All blue at 2-5 kHz  
**Issue:** Too dark, vocals/leads get lost  
**Fix:** Gentle boost at 2.5-3.5 kHz for definition

## Moody Vibe Verification Checklist

Use Spectroman to confirm:

- [ ] **Low-End:** Strong presence at 60-200 Hz (red/yellow)
- [ ] **Warmth:** Visible energy at 200-400 Hz (yellow/green)
- [ ] **Restrained Presence:** 2-5 kHz present but not dominant (green)
- [ ] **Dark Top-End:** Blue/black above 8 kHz
- [ ] **Clean Sub:** Minimal below 30 Hz
- [ ] **Intimate Stereo:** Moderate vectorscope width
- [ ] **Bottom-Heavy Visual:** More energy in lower half of spectrum

## Moody Processing Chain + Spectroman Monitoring

### Suggested Signal Chain

**Pre-Spectroman Processing:**
1. **High-Pass Filter:** 30-40 Hz (clean foundation)
2. **Low-Mid Boost:** +2 dB at 100 Hz, +2 dB at 250 Hz
3. **Presence Control:** -1 to -2 dB at 4 kHz
4. **High-Shelf Cut:** -2 to -3 dB above 10 kHz
5. **Saturation (optional):** Add warmth to low-mids

**Check in Spectroman After Each Step:**
- High-pass: Red below 30 Hz should disappear
- Low-mid boost: Increased red/yellow at 100-250 Hz
- Presence cut: Less red at upper-mids
- High-shelf cut: Darker blue above 10 kHz

## A/B Comparison Tips

### Comparing Your Moody Mix to Reference

**Setup:**
1. Load moody reference track
2. Match levels (LUFS matching essential)
3. Open Spectroman on Master

**What to Compare:**
- **Low-End:** How deep does reference go? How much red at 60-100 Hz?
- **Warmth:** Does reference emphasize 200-400 Hz similarly?
- **Top-End:** How dark is the high-end roll-off?
- **Overall Tilt:** Is reference more bottom-heavy or balanced?

**Adjust Based on Comparison:**
- Reference darker → Cut more highs
- Reference warmer → Boost more low-mids
- Reference deeper → Enhance sub-bass (40-60 Hz)

---

## The Bottom Line

**Moody spectrum should look dark and bottom-heavy.** Strong red/yellow in bass and low-mids (60-400 Hz), controlled presence (2-5 kHz), and rapid roll-off to blue/black in the highs (above 8 kHz). Use Spectroman to verify your moody vibe is translating visually: if it looks bright and top-heavy, it won't feel moody. Embrace the darkness in the high frequencies and the weight in the lows - that's what makes it moody.

**Quick Moody Check:**
- Bottom half of spectrum (20 Hz - 2 kHz) → Should be dominant
- Top half (5 kHz - 20 kHz) → Should be darker and subdued
