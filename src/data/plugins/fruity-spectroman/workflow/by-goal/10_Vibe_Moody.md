# Vibe Workflow: MOODY - Fruity Spectroman

## Moody Vibe Overview

**Sonic Characteristics:**
- Dark, brooding, introspective
- Heavy low-end with subdued highs
- Intimate, weighted atmosphere
- Emotional depth through warmth

**Spectral Signature:**
- Bottom-heavy spectrum
- Strong 60-200 Hz presence
- Dark roll-off above 8 kHz
- Restrained presence zone

---

## Spectroman Configuration for Moody

**Settings:**
- FFT Size: 4096 (capture low-end weight detail)
- Decay: Medium-Slow (see sustained dark character)
- Peak Hold: OFF (average energy view)
- Averaging: Medium (smooth, moody appearance)
- Frequency Range: Full

**Why These Settings:**
- Larger FFT shows warmth and weight in detail
- Slower decay reveals sustained moody character
- Smooth display reflects emotional weight

## Step-by-Step Moody Mix Workflow

### Step 1: Build Dark, Heavy Low-End (5 min)

**Objective:** Establish moody foundation with weight

**Actions:**
1. **Start with bass/808/kick**
2. **Boost 60-120 Hz** for weight and warmth
3. **Check Spectroman:** Should show heavy red at this zone
4. **High-pass at 30-35 Hz** (remove rumble, not bass)

**Spectroman Verification:**
- Heavy red/yellow at 60-200 Hz ✓
- Minimal content below 30 Hz ✓
- This zone dominates the low-end ✓

**Common Adjustments:**
- If too boomy: Cut 80-100 Hz slightly
- If lacks weight: Boost 80 Hz more
- If has rumble: High-pass higher (35-40 Hz)

---

### Step 2: Add Warm Low-Mids (3 min)

**Objective:** Build emotional warmth and body

**Actions:**
1. **Boost 200-300 Hz** on bass, pads, or keys
2. **Add thickness** without mud
3. **Check Spectroman:** Should see yellow/green at 200-400 Hz

**Spectroman Verification:**
- Present at 200-500 Hz (warmth zone) ✓
- Not excessive (avoid mud) ✓
- Adds emotional weight ✓

**Balance Point:**
- Too much = muddy (excessive red at 200-500 Hz)
- Too little = thin (all blue at 200-500 Hz)
- Right amount = warmth without mud (yellow/green)

---

### Step 3: Control Presence Zone (3 min)

**Objective:** Keep vocals/leads clear but not aggressive

**Actions:**
1. **Check 2-5 kHz** in Spectroman
2. **Should be present but restrained** (green/cyan, not red)
3. **If too forward:** Cut 3-4 kHz slightly
4. **If buried:** Gentle boost at 2.5-3 kHz

**Spectroman Verification:**
- Vocals/leads visible at 2-5 kHz ✓
- Not aggressive or in-your-face ✓
- Smooth, controlled presence ✓

**Moody Vocal Character:**
- Clear enough to understand
- Not bright or aggressive
- Intimate and close
- Sits "within" the mix, not "above" it

---

### Step 4: Darken the Top-End (4 min)

**Objective:** Create dark, closed-in atmosphere

**Actions:**
1. **Cut 5-10 kHz** (-2 to -4 dB) for reduced brightness
2. **High-shelf cut above 10 kHz** (-2 to -3 dB) for darkness
3. **Check Spectroman:** Should see blue/black above 8 kHz

**Spectroman Verification:**
- Blue/cyan above 5 kHz ✓
- Blue/black above 10 kHz ✓
- Dark, not dull (some content remains) ✓

**Visual Indicator:**
- Spectrum should fade to blue in highs
- NOT all black (needs some detail)
- Gradual dark roll-off

**Common Mistakes:**
- Cutting too much = dull, lifeless
- Not cutting enough = still too bright
- Right balance = dark but present

---

### Step 5: Maintain Intimate Stereo Field (2 min)

**Objective:** Keep mix intimate, not wide and spacious

**Actions:**
1. **Check vectorscope** during playback
2. **Ensure bass is centered** (vertical component)
3. **Moderate width on supporting elements**
4. **Avoid extreme stereo widening**

**Spectroman Verification:**
- Vectorscope shows moderate oval ✓
- Strong vertical component (bass centered) ✓
- Not overly wide (horizontal pattern) ✓

**Stereo Strategy:**
- Keep bass, kick, vocals mono/centered
- Moderate width on pads, synths
- Use narrow reverb width
- Avoid stereo enhancers on everything

## Moody-Specific EQ Moves

### Boost for Moody Weight

**+2 to +4 dB at 80-120 Hz:**
- Adds warmth and weight
- Check Spectroman: More red in low-end
- Creates moody foundation

**+1 to +2 dB at 200-300 Hz:**
- Adds emotional thickness
- Check Spectroman: Increased warmth zone
- Be careful not to create mud

---

### Cut for Moody Darkness

**-2 to -4 dB at 5-8 kHz:**
- Reduces brightness and sparkle
- Check Spectroman: Less content in highs
- Creates darker character

**High-shelf cut above 10 kHz (-2 to -3 dB):**
- Darkens overall top-end
- Check Spectroman: Fade to blue/black
- Closed-in, intimate feel

**Optional: -1 to -2 dB at 3-4 kHz:**
- Softens presence if too forward
- Check Spectroman: Less red in upper-mids
- More intimate vocal character

## Moody Troubleshooting

### Problem: Mix Sounds Moody But Lacks Clarity

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Dark highs (good for moody) ✓
- Weak at 2-5 kHz (vocals buried) ❌

**Solution:**
- Gentle boost at 2.5-3.5 kHz on vocals
- Just enough for clarity, not brightness
- Check Spectroman: Slight increase in presence zone
- Should remain restrained but clear

---

### Problem: Mix Sounds Dark But Thin

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Blue above 5 kHz (good for dark) ✓
- Weak at 60-200 Hz (lacks weight) ❌

**Solution:**
- Boost 80-120 Hz for weight
- Add warmth at 200-300 Hz
- Check Spectroman: Increased red in low zones

---

### Problem: Mix Sounds Muddy, Not Moody

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Heavy red at 200-500 Hz (too much warmth) ❌
- Dark highs ✓
- Bottom-heavy ✓

**Solution:**
- Cut 300-400 Hz to clean up
- Maintain warmth at 200 Hz and 100 Hz
- Check Spectroman: Less red at mud zone
- Should be warm but clear

## Moody Processing Chain Example

**Suggested Signal Chain:**

1. **High-Pass Filter:** 30-35 Hz (clean foundation)
2. **Low-Mid Boost:** +2 dB at 100 Hz, +2 dB at 250 Hz (weight + warmth)
3. **Mud Control:** -1 to -2 dB at 350 Hz (prevent mud)
4. **Presence Control:** -1 to -2 dB at 4 kHz (soften)
5. **High-Shelf Cut:** -2 to -3 dB above 10 kHz (darkness)
6. **Saturation (optional):** Add warmth to low-mids

**Check in Spectroman After Each Step:**
- High-pass: Red below 30 Hz disappears
- Low-mid boost: More red at 100-250 Hz
- Mud control: Cleaner at 350 Hz
- Presence control: Less forward at 4 kHz
- High-shelf cut: Darker above 10 kHz

---

## The Bottom Line

**Moody mixes are bottom-heavy with dark, subdued tops.** Use Spectroman to verify heavy red at 60-200 Hz (weight), warm yellow/green at 200-400 Hz (emotion), restrained green at 2-5 kHz (clarity without aggression), and blue/black above 8 kHz (darkness). Keep stereo field intimate (moderate width), bass centered (vectorscope vertical), and high-end dark (fade to blue). Compare to moody references at matched levels to calibrate your darkness and weight.

**Moody Spectrum at a Glance:**
- Bottom half (20 Hz - 2 kHz): DOMINANT
- Top half (5 kHz - 20 kHz): SUBDUED AND DARK

**If it looks bright and top-heavy, it won't feel moody.**
