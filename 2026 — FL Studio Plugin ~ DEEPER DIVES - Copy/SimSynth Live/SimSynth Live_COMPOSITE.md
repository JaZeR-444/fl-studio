```markdown
## Purpose
SimSynth Live is a classic subtractive synth with three oscillators, Warm mode, an SVF filter, and simple FX. It excels at thick pads and vintage basses.

## 10-Minute Orientation
1. Enable Osc 1 and Osc 2, set to Saw, turn on Warm.
2. Use SVF mix to blend LP/BP/HP.
3. Add chorus for width.
4. Use LFO to modulate pulse width for motion.
```

```markdown
## 🎯 What is SimSynth Live?

SimSynth Live is a legacy FL Studio plugin modeled after the **Oberheim SEM** and other 80s analog classics. It features 3 oscillators with a unique "Warm" (Dual) mode, allowing for 6-oscillator thickness. It is known for its fat bass sounds, rich strings, and simple but effective SVF (State Variable Filter). It represents the sound of early virtual analog software.

**Key Capabilities:**
- **3 Oscillators:** Saw, Square, Triangle, Sine, Noise.
- **Warm Mode:** Doubling of oscillators for instant detuned thickness.
- **SVF Filter:** Lowpass, Bandpass, Highpass mixing.
- **LFO:** Route to Pitch, Filter, or Pulse Width.
- **Chorus:** Built-in stereo widening.
- **Ring Modulation:** For metallic and bell-like tones.
```

```markdown
# 00: What SimSynth Live Is and When to Use It

## Purpose and Identity
SimSynth Live is a classic subtractive synthesizer inspired by 80s analog giants like the **Oberheim SEM**. It is designed for "weight" and "warmth." Its standout feature is the **Warm Mode**, which doubles the oscillators (totaling 6) to create instant detuned thickness without needing external unison. [SRC: IL-MAN-03]

## Where it Fits in FL Studio
It is a "Legacy" plugin, but don't let the UI fool you. It provides a specific "round" analog tone that is harder to dial in with more modern, digital-sounding synths. It’s a step up from 3x Osc in terms of character but simpler than Sawer.

## When to Use vs. When Not to Use
| Use SimSynth Live When... | Avoid SimSynth Live When... |
| :--- | :--- |
| You need fat, 80s-style analog bass. | You need modern, sharp wavetable leads. |
| You want lush, wide "Warm" pads. | You need complex FM or additive textures. |
| You want to blend LP, BP, and HP filters simultaneously. | You need high-order, clinical digital filtering. |
| You are going for a retro-synthwave or vintage R&B vibe. | You need deep modulation routing (use Sytrus). |

## 60-Second Mental Model: The "SEM" Spirit
Imagine a 1970s hardware module. You have three knobs to mix different filter types (Lowpass, Bandpass, Highpass) at the same time. This creates "hybrid" filter shapes that are rare in other synths. Combine this with "Warm" oscillators, and you have a thick, harmonically rich analog engine.

## Hip-Hop/R&B Context
- **West Coast G-Funk:** Perfect for those whiny, high-pitched "warm" leads.
- **Classic R&B Pads:** Use the built-in Chorus and Warm oscillators for that 90s slow-jam feel.
- **Boom Bap Bass:** The "round" sine and square waves with the LP filter provide a perfect "under-the-kick" bass.
- **80s Pop-Hip-Hop:** Excellent for bright, punchy brass stabs.
```

```markdown
# 01: UI Map and Signal Flow

## UI Sections Tour
SimSynth Live uses a logical, left-to-right signal path that mimics a hardware rack. [SRC: IL-MAN-03]

### 1. Oscillators (1, 2, & 3)
- **Shape:** Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square, Pulse, Noise.
- **Warm Button:** The "Magic" button. It adds a second, detuned oscillator for every one you enable.
- **Level & Tuning:** Balance the volume and pitch of the three sources.

### 2. SVF Section (State Variable Filter)
- **LP / BP / HP Mix:** These are not switches; they are knobs. You can mix 50% Lowpass and 50% Highpass to create a "Notch" filter.
- **Cutoff & Res:** Standard frequency and feedback controls.

### 3. Modulation (LFO & Envelopes)
- **LFO:** Can be routed to Pitch (Vibrato), Filter (Wobble), or PWM (Pulse Width).
- **ADSR:** Two dedicated envelopes—one for Volume (Amp) and one for the Filter.

### 4. Effects (Chorus & Ring Mod)
- **Chorus:** Adds stereo width and "shimmer."
- **Ring Mod:** Multiplies Osc 1 and Osc 2 for metallic, bell-like, or "robotic" textures.

## Signal Flow
`Osc 1/2/3 (with optional Warm doubling) -> Ring Mod -> SVF Filter -> Envelopes -> Chorus -> Output`

## Things Beginners Misunderstand
- **Filter "Mix" Knobs:** Most synths use a switch to pick LP or HP. In SimSynth, if all three (LP/BP/HP) are at 0, you will hear nothing. You MUST turn at least one up.
- **Warm Mode Polyphony:** Turning on "Warm" doubles the internal oscillators. If you have high polyphony, this can hit the CPU harder than expected.
- **Ring Modulation:** This only works between Osc 1 and Osc 2. If one is muted, the ring mod effect disappears.

```

```markdown
# 02: Core Techniques and Best Practices

## 1. The "Warm" Unison
Always start your lead or pad sounds with the **Warm** button engaged on at least two oscillators. This creates the "analog drift" that makes SimSynth sound expensive. For a tight bass, you might keep it off to maintain phase consistency. [SRC: REPUTABLE-04]

## 2. Blending the "Notch" Filter
To create a "Notch" filter (popular in psychedelic or phaser-like sounds):
1. Set the **LP Mix** to 50%.
2. Set the **HP Mix** to 50%.
3. Set the **BP Mix** to 0%.
4. Sweep the **Cutoff**. You’ll hear a "hollow" sound that is perfect for psychedelic sweeps.

## 3. Pulse Width Modulation (PWM)
For that classic "reedy" 80s lead:
1. Select the **Square/Pulse** wave on Osc 1.
2. In the LFO section, route the modulation to **PWM**.
3. Set LFO speed to 3Hz.
- *Result:* The thickness of the square wave will cycle, creating a "moving" analog tone.

## 4. Metallic Bell Tones (Ring Mod)
1. Turn on **Osc 1** (Sine) and **Osc 2** (Sine).
2. Set Osc 2 pitch to +7 or +12 semitones.
3. Turn up the **Ring Mod** amount.
4. Use a fast Decay on the Amp Envelope.
- *Result:* You get a physical-sounding bell or "chime" that is perfect for modern "Vibey" R&B.

## 5. The "Wide" String Layer
- Set Osc 1 and Osc 2 to **Saw**.
- Engagement **Warm** mode on both.
- Turn on the built-in **Chorus**.
- Set a slow **Attack** (500ms).
- *Result:* A massive, cinematic string pad that fills the stereo field.

## 6. Sub-Bass Layering
Use **Osc 3** as a dedicated sub. Set it to **Sine** or **Triangle**, and turn its **Coarse Tune** down -12 semitones. Keep "Warm" off for this oscillator to keep the low-end "mono" and "solid."

## Common Pitfalls + Fixes
- **Pitfall:** Sound is too "thin."
  - **Fix:** Enable "Warm" mode and add a second oscillator detuned by 3-5 cents.
- **Pitfall:** "Clicking" at the start of notes.
  - **Fix:** Increase the **Amp Attack** slightly (5-10ms).
- **Pitfall:** No sound after turning knobs.
  - **Fix:** Check the **SVF Mix knobs**. At least one must be turned up from zero.
```

```markdown
# 03: Vibe Translation Guide

## Vibe Targets in SimSynth Live

### 1. Moody (Dark, Intimate, Emotional)
- **The Lever:** **LP Filter + Triangle Waves.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Use **Triangle** waves for a softer, less "buzzy" foundation.
  - Set **LP Mix** to 100%, Cutoff to 20%.
  - Slow **LFO -> Pitch** (1%) for a "tired" analog drift.
- **What to listen for:** A warm, "felt-like" tone that sits in the background of a mix.
- **Pitfall:** High resonance will make it too "aggressive." Keep Res below 10%.

### 2. Upbeat (Punchy, Bright, 80s Pop)
- **The Lever:** **Saw Waves + Warm Mode + Filter Snap.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - All 3 Oscillators set to **Saw** with **Warm** ON.
  - **Filter Env Amount** at 70%, short **Decay**.
  - **HP Mix** at 10% to cut extreme mud.
- **What to listen for:** A "brass-like" hit that is bright and energetic.
- **Pitfall:** Don't use the Chorus if you want it to be "punchy"—Chorus can sometimes soften transients.

### 3. Psychedelic (Warped, Moving, Evolving)
- **The Lever:** **Ring Mod + SVF Blending.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Blend **LP** and **BP** filters at 50/50.
  - Turn up **Ring Mod** for metallic overtones.
  - Automate the **LFO Speed** from slow to fast.
- **What to listen for:** "Hollow" or "robotic" textures that shift harmonically.
- **Pitfall:** Too much Ring Mod can become unmusical. Use it as a "seasoning."

### 4. Jazzy (Organic, Sophisticated, Clean)
- **The Lever:** **Sine/Square Mix + Soft Attack.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - Mix **Osc 1 (Sine)** and **Osc 2 (Square)** for a "hollow" organ/Rhodes tone.
  - Set **Attack** to 50ms for a "human" touch.
  - Use the **BP filter** to isolate the "warm" mid-range frequencies.
- **What to listen for:** A sound that feels like a vintage keyboard or a clean electric bass.
- **Pitfall:** Avoid the "Noise" shape for Jazzy vibes; keep it clean.

### 5. Vibey (Lush, Wide, Modern R&B)
- **The Lever:** **Warm Mode + Chorus + Detune.**
- **Setting Moves:**
  - All oscillators in **Warm** mode.
  - **Chorus Mix** at 50%.
  - Detune **Osc 2** by +5 cents and **Osc 3** by -5 cents.
- **What to listen for:** An "expansive" sound that feels like it's surrounding the listener.
- **Pitfall:** Check mono compatibility; extreme detuning + chorus can cause "thinness" in mono.
```

```markdown
# Filter Topology: The SVF Spirit

## What is a State Variable Filter (SVF)?
Unlike standard filters that are either Lowpass OR Highpass, an SVF provides simultaneous outputs for all filter types. In SimSynth Live, this is implemented as three "Mix" knobs: **LP, BP, and HP**. [SRC: IL-MAN-03]

## The "SEM" Connection
The Oberheim SEM (Synthesizer Expander Module) used this exact topology. It allowed users to create "continuously variable" filter shapes.
- **Lowpass (LP):** Standard "warm" sound.
- **Bandpass (BP):** Nasal, "wah-wah" sound.
- **Highpass (HP):** Thin, "radio" sound.

## How to use the Mix knobs for "Vibe":
1. **The "Warm" Lowpass:** LP 100%, BP 0%, HP 0%. (The standard choice).
2. **The "Hollow" Lead:** LP 50%, HP 50%. This creates a **Notch Filter**. It sounds ghostly and "scooped," perfect for **Psychedelic** textures.
3. **The "Mid-Punch" Bass:** LP 80%, BP 20%. Adding a little Bandpass to the Lowpass adds a "honk" to the bass that helps it cut through small speakers.
4. **The "Airy" Pad:** HP 50%, LP 20%. This lets the highs through but keeps a subtle "body" from the low-pass. Perfect for **Vibey** R&B background layers.

## Resonance Behavior
The Resonance in SimSynth is relatively "tame" compared to Sawer. It adds a "hollow" whistle without distorting the signal too much, making it very "safe" for **Jazzy** or **Vibey** sounds where you don't want harsh artifacts.
```

```markdown
# Genre Rules: Hip-Hop & R&B

## The "Warm" Low-End Rule
- **Rule:** For the main 808 or sub-bass, **Turn "Warm" mode OFF**. "Warm" creates two oscillators with shifting phases, which causes the sub-bass volume to "waver" or "fade." Keep your sub-bass mono and solid.
- **Rule:** Use **Warm** mode for mid-bass and leads to add the necessary analog thickness.

## Filter Blending for Hip-Hop
- **Rule:** To make a bass cut through on phone speakers, mix in 10-20% **Bandpass (BP)** with your Lowpass. This emphasizes the mid-range "punch."
- **Rule:** For "under-the-vocal" pads, use 10-20% **Highpass (HP)** to clear out the mud without losing the "body" of the sound.

## Transient Control
- **Rule:** SimSynth has very fast envelopes. If your pluck "clicks" too hard for a smooth R&B vibe, increase the **Attack** to 10-20ms.
- **Rule:** For aggressive trap leads, set **Attack** to 0 and use the **Filter Env** to create a sharp "pluck" at the start of every note.

## Stereo Strategy
- **Rule:** SimSynth's built-in **Chorus** is very wide. If using it on a main melody, check your mono compatibility frequently. If it sounds "thin" in mono, reduce the Chorus mix to 25%.
- **Rule:** For "Vibey" background textures, go full 100% Chorus—the phasing in mono won't matter as much for background layers.

## Avoiding "Mud"
- **Rule:** Because you have 3 oscillators, it’s easy to create a messy sound. Keep **Osc 1** as your main tone, and use **Osc 2 and 3** at lower levels (40-60%) for harmonic support.
- **Rule:** If the sound is too "noisy," check if the **Noise** shape is enabled on any oscillator.
```

```markdown
Create a warm, dark, and intimate analog texture that provides a "lonely" atmosphere.

## Step-by-Step Setup
1. **Oscillator:** Osc 1 (Triangle, Warm).
2. **Filter:** LP Mix 100%. Cutoff at 150Hz. Resonance 0%.
3. **Envelopes:** Attack 50ms, Release 1s.

5. **Acoustics:** Chorus Mix 20% (Subtle).

## What to Listen For
- A "felt" or "soft" sound that doesn't have any sharp edges.
- A subtle pitch drift that makes it feel like an old recording.
```

```markdown
## Goal Statement
```

```markdown
# SVF Mathematics & Slopes: The SimSynth Engine

## What makes the SimSynth SVF unique?
Most modern digital filters use "Cascaded Biquads." SimSynth uses a **State Variable Filter (SVF)** topology modeled after the Oberheim SEM. [SRC: IL-MAN-03]

## Mathematical Characteristics:
1. **Parallel Outputs:** Unlike other plugins where you choose "Low Pass OR High Pass," SimSynth calculates both simultaneously and allows you to **MIX** them.
2. **Phase Stability:** SVFs are known for maintaining a more consistent phase relationship across the frequency spectrum, which is why SimSynth bass sounds so "solid."
3. **Slope Behavior:** SimSynth's filter is a 12dB/octave (2-pole) design. 
   - *Compared to 24dB:* It is less "steep." This means it lets more harmonics through, resulting in a "musical" and "smooth" sound rather than a "surgical" one.

## Professional Use Case: The "Soft Notch"
By mixing 50% Low Pass and 50% High Pass, you create a "Notch" filter. 
- **The Research Value:** In a dense Hip-Hop mix, a Notch filter is better than a Low-Pass for pads. It keeps the "bass" and the "air" but clears out the middle frequencies where the vocals and snare live.

```
