## Essential Hip-Hop Frequency Zones

### Zone 1: Sub-Bass (40-80 Hz) - THE PRIORITY

**Spectroman Target:**
- Heavy red/yellow at 40-80 Hz
- THIS is the dominant zone for hip-hop
- Should be THE most prominent visual element

**808 Visual Signature:**
- Clear, sustained red at 40-60 Hz (depending on note)
- Not competing with kick drum
- Mono signal (vectorscope vertical)

**Common Issues:**
- **Too much below 30 Hz:** Wasted energy, high-pass at 30-35 Hz
- **Weak 808:** Boost fundamental, check tuning
- **Overlaps with kick:** Sidechain or tune to different frequencies

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Make 40-80 Hz the heaviest zone  
✅ **DO:** Keep 808 mono (centered in vectorscope)  
✅ **DO:** High-pass everything else at 100+ Hz  
❌ **DON'T:** Let other elements compete in this zone  
❌ **DON'T:** Add stereo width to 808/bass

---

### Zone 2: Low-Mids (200-500 Hz) - KEEP CLEAN

**Spectroman Target:**
- Green/yellow, NOT heavy red
- Much less energy than 40-80 Hz zone
- Clean and controlled

**Why Clean Low-Mids Matter:**
- Muddy low-mids = amateur hip-hop mix
- Clarity comes from controlled 200-500 Hz
- 808 should dominate, not warmth

**Common Issues:**
- **Muddy mix:** Too much red at 200-500 Hz
- **Boomy kick:** Excessive 200-300 Hz

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Cut 300-400 Hz on multiple elements  
✅ **DO:** Keep this zone lighter than bass zone  
✅ **DO:** High-pass synths/samples at 200+ Hz  
❌ **DON'T:** Add warmth to everything  
❌ **DON'T:** Let this zone dominate spectrum

---

### Zone 3: Vocal Clarity (2-5 kHz)

**Spectroman Target:**
- Present and clear (yellow/green)
- Not harsh (no bright red spikes)
- Vocals sit "above" the beat visually

**Vocal Visual Signature:**
- Distinct presence at 2.5-4 kHz
- Should be prominent but smooth
- Not competing with hi-hats (6-10 kHz)

**Common Issues:**
- **Vocals buried:** Weak at 2-5 kHz, boost presence
- **Vocals harsh:** Red spikes at 3-5 kHz, cut harshness
- **Competes with hi-hats:** Separate zones (vocals 2-4 kHz, hats 6-10 kHz)

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Boost vocal presence at 2.5-4 kHz  
✅ **DO:** Cut mud on vocals (200-400 Hz)  
✅ **DO:** De-ess if needed (5-8 kHz)  
❌ **DON'T:** Over-boost (causes harshness)  
❌ **DON'T:** Neglect this zone (vocals get buried)

---

### Zone 4: Hi-Hats & Percussion (6-12 kHz)

**Spectroman Target:**
- Moderate presence (green/cyan)
- Clear but not dominating
- Adds energy without harshness

**Hi-Hat Visual Signature:**
- Content visible at 6-10 kHz
- Should complement vocals, not compete
- Modern trap: extended to 12+ kHz

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Keep hi-hats bright but controlled  
✅ **DO:** High-pass hi-hats at 300-500 Hz  
❌ **DON'T:** Let hi-hats dominate spectrum  
❌ **DON'T:** Clash with vocal sibilance zone

## Hip-Hop Mixing Workflow with Spectroman

### Step 1: Build the 808 Foundation (5 min)

1. Solo 808 + kick
2. Open Spectroman, zoom to 20-200 Hz
3. Check:
   - 808 shows red at 40-80 Hz
   - Kick shows red at 50-80 Hz (or offset from 808)
   - No heavy red below 30 Hz
4. Adjust:
   - High-pass both at 30-35 Hz
   - Sidechain if competing
   - Tune for clarity

**Target:** Clear, dominant low-end in Spectroman

---

### Step 2: Keep Low-Mids Clean (3 min)

1. Add synths, samples to mix
2. Check 200-500 Hz in Spectroman
3. If too much red:
   - Cut 300-400 Hz on multiple elements
   - High-pass non-bass elements higher
4. Target: Less energy here than 40-80 Hz zone

---

### Step 3: Place Vocals (5 min)

1. Add vocals to mix
2. Check 2-5 kHz in Spectroman
3. Should see distinct presence
4. If buried:
   - Boost 2.5-4 kHz on vocals
   - Cut 2-4 kHz slightly on competing elements
5. Target: Vocals clearly visible and prominent

---

### Step 4: Add Hi-Hats & Percussion (3 min)

1. Add hi-hats, percussion
2. Check 6-12 kHz in Spectroman
3. Should add energy without dominating
4. Target: Moderate presence, complements vocals

---

### Step 5: Final Balance Check (2 min)

**Full Spectrum Check:**
- Heaviest at 40-80 Hz ✓
- Clean at 200-500 Hz ✓
- Clear at 2-5 kHz (vocals) ✓
- Present at 6-12 kHz (hi-hats) ✓

**Vectorscope Check:**
- 808/kick centered (vertical) ✓
- Moderate width overall ✓
- Not extreme stereo ✓

## R&B Spectral Profile

### Characteristic Visual Signature

**What Professional R&B Looks Like:**

```
20-40 Hz:    ▓ (minimal, clean)
40-80 Hz:    ████ (controlled bass, less than trap)
80-200 Hz:   ██████ (warmth and body)
200-500 Hz:  ████ (warm low-mids, more than hip-hop)
500-2k Hz:   ██████ (musical fullness)
2k-5k Hz:    ████████ (vocal intimacy and clarity)
5k-10k Hz:   ████ (air and detail)
10k-20k Hz:  ██ (subtle sparkle)
```

**Key Differences from Hip-Hop:**
- More warmth in 200-500 Hz (emotional weight)
- Less dominant sub-bass (40-80 Hz)
- More emphasis on vocal zone (2-5 kHz)
- Smoother overall (less harsh)

---

## R&B Frequency Zone Rules

### Zone 1: Controlled Bass (40-100 Hz)

**Spectroman Target:**
- Present but NOT dominating like trap
- Yellow/green, not heavy red
- Supports rather than leads

**R&B Bass Visual:**
- Moderate presence at 60-100 Hz
- Less extended sub than hip-hop
- Warm and musical, not aggressive

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Keep bass controlled and musical  
✅ **DO:** Focus on warmth over weight  
❌ **DON'T:** Make bass as heavy as trap  
❌ **DON'T:** Over-boost sub-bass

---

### Zone 2: Warmth & Emotion (200-500 Hz)

**Spectroman Target:**
- More present than hip-hop (yellow zone)
- Adds emotional weight and intimacy
- Warm but not muddy

**Why R&B Needs Warmth:**
- Emotional, intimate character
- Vocal richness
- Musical fullness

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Accept more 200-500 Hz than hip-hop  
✅ **DO:** Use warmth for emotional impact  
✅ **DO:** Check it's warmth, not mud  
❌ **DON'T:** Cut all low-mids (loses emotion)  
❌ **DON'T:** Let it become muddy

---

### Zone 3: Vocal Intimacy (2-5 kHz)

**Spectroman Target:**
- Prominent and smooth (yellow/red)
- Most important zone for R&B
- Intimate and clear, not harsh

**R&B Vocal Visual:**
- Strong presence at 2-4 kHz
- Smooth curve, not spikes
- Sits prominently in mix

**Rules:**
✅ **DO:** Make vocals THE focal point  
✅ **DO:** Boost 2-4 kHz for intimacy  
✅ **DO:** Keep smooth (avoid harshness)  
❌ **DON'T:** Let vocals get buried  
❌ **DON'T:** Over-boost (causes fatigue)

## Common Hip-Hop Mixing Mistakes (Spectroman Evidence)

### Mistake 1: Weak 808

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Blue/cyan at 40-80 Hz instead of red
- Other elements dominate low-end

**Fix:**
- Boost 808 level or fundamental frequency
- High-pass everything else more aggressively
- Check 808 tuning

---

### Mistake 2: Muddy Low-Mids

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Heavy red at 200-500 Hz
- Competes with or exceeds 40-80 Hz presence

**Fix:**
- Cut 300-400 Hz on multiple tracks
- High-pass synths/pads at 200-300 Hz
- Clean up low-mids systematically

---

### Mistake 3: Buried Vocals

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Weak at 2-5 kHz
- Beat elements dominate this zone

**Fix:**
- Boost vocals at 2.5-4 kHz
- Cut competing elements in this zone
- Check vocal level in mix

---

### Mistake 4: Phase Issues in Bass

**Spectroman Shows:**
- Vectorscope shows horizontal pattern
- Bass disappears in mono

**Fix:**
- Make 808/kick mono
- Remove stereo processing on bass
- Test mono compatibility

## Reference Comparison Tips

### Choosing Hip-Hop References

**Modern Trap:** Metro Boomin, Southside, ATL Jacob  
**Look for:** Heavy 40-80 Hz, clean low-mids, clear vocals

**Boom Bap:** DJ Premier, 9th Wonder  
**Look for:** Less sub-bass, more mid-range punch

**When Comparing:**
1. Match LUFS levels first
2. Note how heavy the 40-80 Hz zone is
3. Check how clean 200-500 Hz is
4. Verify vocal placement at 2-5 kHz

### Choosing R&B References

**Contemporary R&B:** The Weeknd, H.E.R., SZA  
**Look for:** Warm low-mids, prominent vocals, smooth spectrum

**When Comparing:**
1. Match levels
2. Note warmth in 200-500 Hz (more than trap)
3. Check vocal prominence at 2-5 kHz
4. Observe smoother overall profile

## The Bottom Line

**Hip-hop/trap spectrum is bass-dominant with clean mids - heavy red at 40-80 Hz, controlled 200-500 Hz, clear 2-5 kHz for vocals.** R&B is warmer with more low-mid presence and vocal intimacy. Use Spectroman to verify your 808 dominates (40-80 Hz), low-mids stay clean (200-500 Hz not muddy), and vocals cut through (2-5 kHz present). Check vectorscope to ensure bass is centered. Compare to genre-appropriate references at matched levels.

**Hip-Hop Golden Rule:** If 40-80 Hz isn't the heaviest zone in your spectrum, your mix isn't hitting hard enough.

**R&B Golden Rule:** Vocals should be the most prominent element at 2-5 kHz, supported by warmth at 200-500 Hz.
