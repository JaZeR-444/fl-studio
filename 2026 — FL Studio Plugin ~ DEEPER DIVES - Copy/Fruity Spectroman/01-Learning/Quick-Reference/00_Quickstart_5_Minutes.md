# Quickstart - 5 Minutes to Using Fruity Spectroman

## What This Plugin Does (10 Seconds)

**Fruity Spectroman** is a **real-time spectrum analyzer** that visually displays your audio's frequency content from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. It doesn't change your audio - it just shows you what's happening across the frequency spectrum.

**Use it to:** Spot frequency buildups, check low-end content, compare to reference tracks, verify stereo imaging.

---

## Setup (30 Seconds)

### Quick Load

**Step 1:** Add to your Master channel
- Mixer → Master track → Add effect → Fruity Spectroman

**Step 2:** Position for visibility
- Drag window to visible location
- Resize if needed for clear view

**Done.** It's now analyzing your mix in real-time.

---

## The Display (1 Minute)

### What You're Looking At

**Top Section (Spectrum Analyzer):**
- **Horizontal axis:** Frequency (20 Hz on left → 20 kHz on right)
- **Vertical axis:** Amplitude (louder = higher on screen)
- **Colors:** 
  - Red/Yellow = Loud frequencies
  - Green/Cyan = Moderate
  - Blue/Black = Quiet or absent

**Bottom Section (Vectorscope):**
- Shows stereo field width
- Vertical line = Mono
- Circle/oval = Stereo width

## Basic Usage (2 Minutes)

### What to Look For

#### 1. **Overall Balance**
Play your track and observe the spectrum:

```
✅ GOOD: Energy distributed across most of the spectrum
❌ BAD: Heavy red in one zone, black everywhere else
```

#### 2. **Low-End Check**
Focus on left side (20-200 Hz):

```
✅ GOOD: Moderate red/yellow at 40-100 Hz (bass/kick)
❌ BAD: Heavy red below 30 Hz (wasted energy/rumble)
```

#### 3. **Clarity Zone**
Check mid-range (2-5 kHz):

```
✅ GOOD: Visible yellow/green (vocal/lead presence)
❌ BAD: All blue (lack of clarity)
```

#### 4. **Air/Sparkle**
Check high end (8-20 kHz):

```
✅ GOOD: Some content visible (air and detail)
❌ BAD: All black (dull, closed-in sound)
```

## Common Issues & Quick Fixes (1.5 Minutes)

### Problem: Too much red below 40 Hz
**What it means:** Wasted energy, won't translate to small speakers  
**Fix:** Add high-pass filter at 30-40 Hz

### Problem: Heavy red at 200-500 Hz
**What it means:** Muddy, boomy mix  
**Fix:** EQ cut in this range (-2 to -4 dB)

### Problem: No content at 2-5 kHz
**What it means:** Lack of vocal/lead clarity  
**Fix:** Boost presence zone (+2 dB at 3 kHz)

### Problem: All blue above 8 kHz
**What it means:** Dull, lifeless top end  
**Fix:** Add air shelf (+1-2 dB at 10 kHz)

## Reference Track Comparison (Quick Method)

**Step 1:** Load a pro track in your genre (separate channel)  
**Step 2:** Match volumes (roughly equal loudness)  
**Step 3:** Play reference, observe spectrum for 20 seconds  
**Step 4:** Play your mix, compare immediately  
**Step 5:** Note major differences (don't try to copy exactly)

**What to Compare:**
- Overall spectral tilt (bright vs. warm)
- Low-end extension (how deep does bass go?)
- Mid-range density (how much energy in 500 Hz - 5 kHz?)
- High-frequency air (content above 10 kHz?)

---

## Pro Tips (30 Seconds)

### DO:
✅ Use Spectroman to **confirm** what you hear  
✅ Check periodically, not constantly  
✅ Focus on glaring issues, not micro-details  
✅ Compare to reference tracks in your genre

### DON'T:
❌ Mix with your eyes instead of ears  
❌ Try to achieve "flat" spectrum (not musical)  
❌ Over-analyze small differences  
❌ Ignore your ears if it sounds good

## You're Ready!

**That's it.** Spectroman is now showing you what your mix is doing frequency-wise. Use it to:
1. Spot obvious imbalances
2. Verify low-end content
3. Learn from reference tracks

**Remember:** Your ears are primary, Spectroman is your visual assistant. If it sounds good, it is good - the spectrum should confirm that, not dictate it.
