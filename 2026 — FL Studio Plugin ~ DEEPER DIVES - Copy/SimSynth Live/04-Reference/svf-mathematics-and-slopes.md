# SVF Mathematics & Slopes: The SimSynth Engine

## What makes the SimSynth SVF unique?
Most modern digital filters use "Cascaded Biquads." SimSynth uses a **State Variable Filter (SVF)** topology modeled after the Oberheim SEM. [SRC: IL-MAN-03]

## Mathematical Characteristics:
1. **Parallel Outputs:** Unlike other plugins where you choose "Low Pass OR High Pass," SimSynth calculates both simultaneously and allows you to **MIX** them.
2. **Phase Stability:** SVFs are known for maintaining a more consistent phase relationship across the frequency spectrum, which is why SimSynth bass sounds so "solid."
3. **Slope Behavior:** SimSynth's filter is a 12dB/octave (2-pole) design. 
   - *Compared to 24dB:* It is less "steep." This means it lets more harmonics through, resulting in a "musical" and "smooth" sound rather than a "surgical" one.

## Professional Use Case: The "Soft Notch"
By mixing 50% Low Pass and 50% High Pass, you create a "Notch" filter. 
- **The Research Value:** In a dense Hip-Hop mix, a Notch filter is better than a Low-Pass for pads. It keeps the "bass" and the "air" but clears out the middle frequencies where the vocals and snare live.
