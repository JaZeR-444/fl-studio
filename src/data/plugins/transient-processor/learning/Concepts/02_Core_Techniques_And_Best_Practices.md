# 02: Core Techniques and Best Practices

## 1. The "Modern Trap Snare" Pop
1. Put Transient Processor on your snare.
2. Turn **Attack** to +6dB.
3. Turn **Release** to -30%.
4. Set **Drive** to 5%.
- *Result:* The snare will have a sharp "click" that cuts through the loudest 808s, while the shorter release keeps the mix clean. [SRC: REPUTABLE-11]

## 2. De-Reverberation (Clean Samples)
If you have a soul sample or vocal with too much room reverb:
1. Turn **Release** to -60% or more.
2. Watch the **Histogram** to ensure the "tail" of the processing is cutting the reverb.
3. Use the **Mix** knob to find the natural balance.

## 3. Fattening the 808 "Knock"
1. Put Transient Processor BEFORE your distortion.
2. Set **Split Freq** to 200Hz (to protect the sub).
3. Boost **Attack** to +4dB.
- *Result:* The initial "thump" of the 808 hits the distortion plugin harder, creating a more aggressive "knock."

## 4. Parallel Drum Bus Glue
1. Route all drums to a bus.
2. Set Transient Processor **Attack** to +10dB (Extreme).
3. Set **Mix** to 20%.
- *Result:* This adds a "layer" of sharpness to the whole kit without making the drums sound over-processed.

## 5. Smoothing "Pokey" Guitars
If an acoustic guitar is too "clicky" or distracting:
1. Turn **Attack** to -4dB.
2. Boost **Release** to +3dB.
- *Result:* This softens the pick noise and emphasizes the "body" and resonance of the strings.

## 6. Protecting the Low-End
Always check your **Split Freq** knob. 
- For Kicks: 100-150Hz.
- For Snares: 200Hz.
- For Melodies: 300Hz+.
- This prevents the transient engine from "fluttering" the sub-frequencies, which sounds like digital distortion.

## Common Pitfalls + Fixes
- **Pitfall:** "Digital distortion/crackling."
  - **Fix:** Increase the **Split Freq** or lower the **Drive**.
- **Pitfall:** "The sound is too dry/dead."
  - **Fix:** You have reduced the **Release** too much. Bring it back toward 0%.
- **Pitfall:** "The effect is too drastic."
  - **Fix:** Use the **Mix** knob at 30-50% for parallel processing.
