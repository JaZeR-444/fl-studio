# Genre & Style Board - Fruity Spectroman

## Visual Reference Guide for Genre-Specific Spectral Profiles

This guide provides quick visual reference for what different genres and styles should look like in Fruity Spectroman.

---

## Hip-Hop / Trap

### Visual Profile
```
SPECTRUM SHAPE:
20-40 Hz:    ██ (clean, high-pass applied)
40-80 Hz:    ██████████████ (DOMINANT - 808/sub-bass)
80-200 Hz:   ████ (bass harmonics, controlled)
200-500 Hz:  ██ (CLEAN - not muddy)
500-2k Hz:   ████ (moderate body)
2k-5k Hz:    ██████ (vocal clarity, hi-hats)
5k-10k Hz:   ████ (hi-hats, percussion)
10k+ Hz:     ██ (subtle air)
```

**Key Characteristics:**
- 40-80 Hz is THE heaviest zone
- Very clean 200-500 Hz (no mud)
- Clear vocal presence at 2-5 kHz
- Moderate stereo width

**Reference Artists:**
Metro Boomin, Southside, ATL Jacob, Pi'erre Bourne

**Mixing Priority:**
1. 808 dominates low-end
2. Clean low-mids
3. Vocal clarity

## Using the Style Board

### For Reference Comparison
1. Identify your target genre
2. Note its characteristic spectral profile
3. Load reference tracks in that genre
4. Compare your mix to the profile

### For Mixing Decisions
1. Check your mix against genre profile
2. Identify deviations
3. Decide if deviations are intentional or problems
4. Adjust toward genre norm or maintain unique character

### For Learning
1. Load professional tracks in different genres
2. Observe how profiles differ
3. Build mental library of spectral signatures
4. Apply knowledge to your own mixes

---

## The Bottom Line

**Each genre has a characteristic spectral fingerprint.** Hip-hop is bass-dominant with clean mids, R&B is vocal-forward and warm, EDM is extended and clean, rock is mid-focused, jazz is natural and warm, lo-fi is thick and dark, dubstep has massive sub-bass. Use this style board to understand what your genre should look like in Spectroman, then mix toward that profile while maintaining your unique character.

**Don't copy exactly - understand the patterns and adapt to your music.**
