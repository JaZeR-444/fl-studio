# Fruity Scratcher - Genre Rules: Hip-Hop & R&B

## Hip-Hop/R&B Context for Fruity Scratcher

Fruity Scratcher exists because of **turntablism culture** in hip-hop. Understanding the genre context is critical for authentic use. This guide covers production rules, cultural considerations, and subgenre-specific approaches.

---

## Universal Hip-Hop/R&B Scratcher Rules

### Rule 1: Respect Turntablism Culture
**Context:** Scratching originated with hip-hop DJs (Grandmaster Flash, Kool Herc). It's not just a "sound effect"—it's a performance art.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Study classic scratch techniques (baby scratch, chirp, flare, transformer)
- ✅ Reference legendary DJ battles and albums (Q-Bert, Mix Master Mike, DJ Shadow)
- ✅ Understand that scratching has **vocabulary** and **technique names**
- ❌ Don't use scratching arbitrarily—it should enhance the track, not clutter it

**Sources:** [REPUTABLE: Turntablism documentaries, DJ technique manuals]

---

### Rule 2: Scratching is a Spice, Not the Main Ingredient
**Context:** Even in scratch-heavy boom-bap, scratches accent the beat—they don't replace it.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Use scratches in breaks, transitions, and hooks
- ✅ Keep scratching minimal in verses (unless it's a scratch solo)
- ✅ Let scratches "breathe"—don't over-scratch
- ❌ Don't scratch constantly throughout a track (listener fatigue)

**Typical scratch density:**
- **Boom-bap:** 2-4 scratches per 8 bars (sparse, intentional)
- **Trap:** 1-2 tape stops per track + occasional cuts
- **R&B:** Minimal scratching, mostly vocal ad-lib cuts

---

### Rule 3: Mono Compatibility is Critical
**Context:** Hip-hop/R&B tracks must work on **all playback systems** (phones, cars, clubs, earbuds).

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Check scratches in mono before finalizing
- ✅ Avoid wide stereo enhancement on scratch channels
- ✅ Center scratch elements in the stereo field
- ❌ Don't use stereo widening unless checking mono fold-down

**How to check:**
- Mixer → Master → Stereo separation knob → Set to 0% (mono)
- Scratches should remain audible and punchy

---

### Rule 4: Transient Preservation is Key
**Context:** Hip-hop/R&B relies on **punch** and **attack** (kicks, snares, scratches).

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Use normalized samples with strong transients
- ✅ Avoid over-compression on scratch channels
- ✅ Use limiters instead of compressors (preserve attack)
- ❌ Don't crush transients with heavy processing

**Safe compression settings (if needed):**
- Ratio: 2:1 to 3:1 (gentle)
- Attack: 10-20 ms (slow—let transient through)
- Release: 100-200 ms (moderate)
- Threshold: -10 to -6 dB

---

### Rule 5: Low-End Discipline
**Context:** In hip-hop/R&B, **808s and bass dominate the low end**. Scratches must not clash.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ High-pass filter scratch channels below 80-100 Hz (unless scratching bass itself)
- ✅ Leave low end for 808s, kick, and bass
- ✅ EQ scratches to fit in midrange (200 Hz - 5 kHz)
- ❌ Don't scratch low-frequency material without EQ

**Recommended EQ for vocal scratches:**
- High-pass: 80-100 Hz (cut rumble)
- Boost: 200-500 Hz (warmth, body)
- Cut: 2-4 kHz (harshness, if present)
- Boost: 5-8 kHz (air, presence)

## Subgenre-Specific Rules

### Boom-Bap (Classic Hip-Hop)

**Context:** 85-95 BPM, sample-based, breakbeat-driven, DJ-centric culture.

**Scratch rules:**
1. **Use classic vocal stabs**
   - "Hey!", "Fresh!", "Yeah!", "Check it out!"
   - Dry, punchy, minimal FX

2. **Scratch on the 2 and 4 (with snare)**
   - Reinforces backbeat
   - Creates call-and-response with drums

3. **Use direct-drive turntable settings**
   - ACC = 75-85% (Technics 1200 feel)
   - SEN = 55-65% (responsive but controlled)

4. **Keep it authentic**
   - Reference DJ Premier, Pete Rock, Q-Bert
   - Avoid over-processing (no heavy reverb/delay)
   - Use vinyl crackle/dust (Effector) sparingly

5. **Scratch in breaks, not verses**
   - Verses = rap focus
   - Hooks/bridges = scratch solos

**Don't:**
- ❌ Use modern trap-style tape stops (wrong aesthetic)
- ❌ Over-process with reverb/delay (boom-bap is dry and punchy)
- ❌ Ignore swing and groove (boom-bap has laid-back feel)

---

### Trap (Modern Hip-Hop)

**Context:** 140 BPM (half-time feel), 808-driven, hi-hat rolls, dark/aggressive energy.

**Scratch rules:**
1. **Tape stops are king**
   - Use on 808s, hi-hats, full beat
   - Gradual stops: ACC = 25-35% (2-4 beats before drop)
   - Quick stops: ACC = 85-95% (instant brake)

2. **Minimal turntablism scratching**
   - Trap is NOT DJ culture-focused
   - Use scratches as FX, not solos

3. **Scratch hi-hats and rolls**
   - Quick cuts on hi-hat triplets
   - Syncopated scratches between rolls

4. **Automate, don't perform**
   - Trap production is programmed, not live
   - Use automation clips for repeatable effects

5. **Pitch drops on 808s**
   - Automate SPD from 1x to 0.6x on 808 tail
   - Creates "falling" bass effect

**Don't:**
- ❌ Use warm, jazzy samples (trap is cold and aggressive)
- ❌ Scratch vocals constantly (trap vocals are already heavily processed)
- ❌ Use belt-drive turntable feel (trap wants tight, digital response)

---

### R&B / Pop-Hip-Hop

**Context:** 70-110 BPM, vocal-centric, melodic, polished production.

**Scratch rules:**
1. **Minimal and tasteful**
   - Scratches are background FX, not focal points
   - Use on ad-libs ("Yeah," "Ooh"), not lead vocals

2. **Pitch drops on vocal endings**
   - Automate SPD from 1x to 0.7x on last syllable of phrase
   - Creates "sinking" emotional effect

3. **Use reverse textures**
   - SPD = -0.6x to -0.8x on pads or vocal hums
   - Creates atmospheric background
   - Heavy reverb (60% mix)

4. **Vibey, not aggressive**
   - ACC = 35-50% (smooth, loose)
   - Avoid rapid scratching

5. **Blend with melodic elements**
   - Scratches should complement chords and melodies
   - Use pitch-matched samples (tuned to song key)

**Don't:**
- ❌ Scratch lead vocals aggressively (breaks R&B smoothness)
- ❌ Use harsh, dry scratches (R&B needs reverb/delay)
- ❌ Ignore melodic context (scratches should fit harmonically)

---

### Lo-Fi Hip-Hop / Chillhop

**Context:** 70-90 BPM, jazzy samples, vinyl texture, laid-back vibe.

**Scratch rules:**
1. **Texture over technique**
   - Use SPD wobble (0.9x ↔ 1.1x) for vinyl warble
   - ACC = 30-40% (loose, analog)
   - Pair with Effector (vinyl mode)

2. **Lazy tape stops**
   - SPD 1x → 0.7x (don't reach full stop)
   - ACC = 25-35%
   - Creates "leaning back" feel

3. **Swung timing**
   - Don't scratch on exact grid
   - Offset scratches ±5-10 ms for human feel

4. **Use jazz/soul samples**
   - Warm, vintage-sounding vocals
   - Piano, Rhodes, upright bass

5. **Minimal FX, maximum vibe**
   - Light reverb (20-30% mix)
   - Parametric EQ 2: Boost 200-500 Hz, cut 8 kHz+

**Don't:**
- ❌ Use bright, clean samples (lo-fi needs dust and warmth)
- ❌ Scratch aggressively (breaks laid-back vibe)
- ❌ Use high ACC (lo-fi wants loose, belt-drive feel)

---

### Drill (Aggressive Hip-Hop Subgenre)

**Context:** 140-150 BPM, dark/menacing, UK/NY/Chicago styles, sliding 808s.

**Scratch rules:**
1. **Use scratches sparingly**
   - Drill is minimalist—scratches are accents only
   - Tape stops on 808 slides

2. **Dark sample selection**
   - Minor-key vocals
   - Aggressive "grr" or "boom" samples

3. **Quick, digital tape stops**
   - ACC = 90-100% (instant, no organic feel)
   - Use on breakdowns before beat switch

4. **No classic turntablism**
   - Drill is not DJ culture; avoid baby scratch, chirp, etc.

**Don't:**
- ❌ Use warm, jazzy samples (wrong aesthetic)
- ❌ Scratch melodically (drill is percussive and cold)

## Sample Selection Rules for Hip-Hop/R&B

### Vocal Sample Guidelines

**DO use:**
- ✅ Dry, punchy vocals (no reverb/delay baked in)
- ✅ Classic hip-hop phrases: "Fresh!", "Yeah!", "Check it!", "Hey!"
- ✅ Ad-libs from soul/funk records (James Brown, Parliament)
- ✅ Short stabs (0.5-2 seconds)

**DON'T use:**
- ❌ Lead vocals from copyrighted songs (legal risk)
- ❌ Overly processed vocals (heavy autotune, reverb)
- ❌ Long vocal phrases (> 3 seconds for scratching)

---

### Drum Sample Guidelines

**DO use:**
- ✅ Dry kick and snare hits
- ✅ Classic breakbeats (Amen, Apache, Funky Drummer)
- ✅ 808 one-shots (for pitch scratching)

**DON'T use:**
- ❌ Overcompressed drums (lose punch when scratched)
- ❌ Reverb-heavy drums (mud up the mix)

---

### Loop Sample Guidelines (Jazz, Soul, Funk)

**DO use:**
- ✅ Warm, analog-sounding loops
- ✅ Piano, Rhodes, upright bass, horns
- ✅ Loops with swing/groove (not quantized)

**DON'T use:**
- ❌ Overly bright/digital loops (wrong vibe)
- ❌ Loops with heavy sub-bass (clashes with 808s)

---

## Timing and Groove Rules

### Rule 1: Respect the Pocket
**Context:** Hip-hop/R&B groove is often "behind the beat" or "laid-back."

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Don't quantize scratches to exact grid
- ✅ Offset scratches +5 to +15 ms for laid-back feel (boom-bap, lo-fi)
- ✅ Offset scratches -5 to -10 ms for "pushing" feel (trap, drill)
- ❌ Don't scratch exactly on grid (sounds robotic)

**How to offset:**
1. Automate scratches or record performance
2. Nudge audio clips left/right in playlist
3. Or use manual scratching with "feel" (not clicking to grid)

---

### Rule 2: Swing Timing for Jazzy/Lo-Fi
**Context:** Jazzy hip-hop uses swing (uneven 16th notes).

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Enable swing in FL Studio (70-80% swing for jazzy feel)
- ✅ Manually offset second scratch of each pair
- ❌ Don't scratch straight 16ths in jazzy context

**How to apply swing:**
- Tools → Macros → Riff machine → Swing = 70%
- Or manually delay second scratch by ~30-50 ms

---

### Rule 3: Tempo-Locked Effects
**Context:** Tape stops, reverse swells, and pitch bends should align with tempo.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Automate SPD over musical divisions (1 beat, 2 beats, 4 beats, 1 bar)
- ✅ End tape stops/swells EXACTLY on downbeats
- ❌ Don't let effects drift off-grid

---

## Mix Integration Rules

### Rule 1: Scratches Must Cut Through
**Context:** Hip-hop/R&B mixes are dense (808s, vocals, drums, samples).

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Boost 3-5 kHz on scratch channel (presence)
- ✅ Use sidechain compression if scratches clash with vocals
- ✅ Pan scratches slightly left/right for space (10-20% pan)
- ❌ Don't bury scratches in the mix

**Suggested EQ for vocal scratches:**
- High-pass: 100 Hz
- Boost: 3-5 kHz (+2 to +4 dB, bell/peaking)
- Cut: 2-3 kHz (-1 to -2 dB, if harsh)

---

### Rule 2: Mono Compatibility (Again, It's Critical)
**Context:** Most hip-hop/R&B listeners use phone speakers, earbuds, cars.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ ALWAYS check mono before exporting
- ✅ Center scratches or use narrow stereo (< 30% width)
- ✅ Avoid phase-cancelled scratches in mono

**Quick mono test:**
- Mixer → Master → Stereo separation = 0%
- If scratches disappear or sound thin, they're phase-problematic

---

### Rule 3: Headroom for Mastering
**Context:** Hip-hop/R&B tracks get loud in mastering (heavy limiting).

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Leave -6 dB headroom on Scratcher channels
- ✅ Use Fruity Limiter with -3 dB ceiling
- ✅ Don't clip during scratch performance
- ❌ Don't mix scratches hot (leaves no room for mastering engineer)

---

## Cultural and Legal Considerations

### Sample Clearance
**Legal context:** Scratching copyrighted vocals is a legal gray area.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Use royalty-free sample packs (Splice, Loopmasters, etc.)
- ✅ Record original vocals for scratching
- ✅ Clear samples if releasing commercially
- ❌ Don't use copyrighted acapellas without permission

**Resources:**
- Harry Fox Agency (sample clearance)
- Creative Commons licensed samples
- Original recording sessions

---

### Respecting Hip-Hop Culture
**Context:** Turntablism is a respected art form with history and pioneers.

**Production implications:**
- ✅ Study classic scratch battles (DMC Championships)
- ✅ Credit scratch techniques to originators (if known)
- ✅ Understand that scratching has vocabulary and rules
- ❌ Don't trivialize scratching as "just a sound effect"
