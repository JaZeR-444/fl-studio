# What It Is And When To Use: Sytrus

## Purpose & Identity
Sytrus is FL Studio's versatile **hybrid synthesizer**. Its primary identity is a powerhouse combining **FM (Frequency Modulation)**, **RM (Ring Modulation)**, **Subtractive**, and **Additive** synthesis. It is the architect of many "Classic" FL sounds, capable of anything from digital glass bells to deep, chest-rattling sub-basses. [SRC: IL-MAN]

## 60-Second Mental Model
Think of Sytrus as a "Signal Lab." You have 6 Operators (oscillators). Instead of just playing them side-by-side, you can use the **Matrix** to have Operator 1 "vibrate" Operator 2 at incredibly high speeds. This creates new harmonics that a standard synth can't touch.

## Hip-Hop / R&B Context
- **The "West Coast" Lead:** Using a single high-pitched Sine wave with portamento for that classic G-Funk whistle (Upbeat).
- **Modern R&B Rhodes:** Using the harmonics editor to build a warm, bell-like electric piano from scratch (Jazzy/Vibey).
- **Nocturnal Bass:** Using FM feedback on Operator 1 to create a "Reese" bass that sounds dark and wide (Moody).

## When To Use
- When you need **metallic, glass, or bell-like** textures.
- When you want to design **custom sub-basses** with surgical control over harmonics.
- When you need a synth with a **massive unison engine** for wide, lush pads.

## When NOT To Use
- **Quick Tweaking:** If you just want a simple Sawtooth with one filter, **3x Osc** is faster. Sytrus requires a "deep dive" for almost every patch.
- **CPU Intensive Pads:** High unison counts (9 voices) can be heavy on older systems. [SRC: REPUTABLE]
