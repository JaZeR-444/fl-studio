# 00: What Sawer Is and When to Use It

## Purpose and Identity
Sawer is a subtractive synthesizer that meticulously emulates the **Polivoks**, a Soviet-era analog synth. Its identity is defined by "imperfection"—it reproduces the unstable voltage behavior, the grit of the hardware oscillators, and most importantly, the unique, aggressive character of the Polivoks filter. [SRC: IL-MAN-02]

## Where it Fits in FL Studio
It is the "Analog Rebel" of the FL Studio lineup. Where plugins like Harmless or Sytrus provide clean, surgical digital synthesis, Sawer provides raw, harmonically rich, and slightly unpredictable analog tones.

## When to Use vs. When Not to Use
| Use Sawer When... | Avoid Sawer When... |
| :--- | :--- |
| You need "stinky" or "gritty" analog bass. | You need clean, pristine digital FM bells. |
| You want 80s synthwave or vintage lo-fi vibes. | You need hyper-modern, complex "Serum-style" wavetables. |
| You want a filter that screams when the resonance is high. | You need a subtle, transparent filter that doesn't color the sound. |
| You need a simple, fast UI for sound design. | You need deep, multi-operator FM routing. |

## 60-Second Mental Model: The "Soviet Grit"
Think of Sawer as a vintage engine that leaks a little oil. The Sawtooth wave isn't a perfect triangle; it has "jitter." The filter doesn't just cut frequencies; it adds saturation and bite. Every parameter is designed to add "warmth" (controlled distortion) to the signal.

## Hip-Hop/R&B Context
- **Trap Bass:** Use the Sub-oscillator for massive, slightly distorted low-end.
- **Moody R&B Pads:** Use the Unison and the built-in Chorus for that "drifting" 80s feel.
- **Lo-Fi Melodies:** The "unstable" nature of the oscillators makes it perfect for "dusty" keys.
- **West Coast Leads:** The Hard Sync is perfect for those high-pitched "whine" leads.
