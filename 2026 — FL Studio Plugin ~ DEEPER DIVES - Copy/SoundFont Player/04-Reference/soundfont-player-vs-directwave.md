# SoundFont Player vs. DirectWave: Which Sampler to Use?

## SoundFont Player (The Specialist)
- **Best For:** Quick, low-CPU playback of existing `.sf2` libraries.
- **Pros:** Extremely fast loading, simple UI, very light on system resources.
- **Cons:** Only supports SF2, very limited synthesis controls, no internal sample editing.
- **The "Vibe":** Simple, retro, and efficient.

## DirectWave (The Powerhouse)
- **Best For:** Professional sampling, creating your own instruments, and playing complex `.sfz` or `.dwp` libraries.
- **Pros:** Deep synthesis (16-slot matrix), multi-layer support, internal wave editor, VST sampling.
- **Cons:** Higher CPU usage, complex UI, slower to set up for simple tasks.
- **The "Vibe":** Professional, deep, and surgical.

## When to use SFP:
1. You just want to play a "Grand Piano" or "Strings" patch from a classic bank.
2. You are working on a laptop with limited CPU.
3. You want the specific "Crunchy" resampling character of the player.

## When to use DirectWave:
1. You want to sample your own external VSTs.
2. You need complex modulation (e.g., Velocity to Pitch).
3. You are building a professional, multi-gigabyte custom library. [SRC: IL-MAN-05]
