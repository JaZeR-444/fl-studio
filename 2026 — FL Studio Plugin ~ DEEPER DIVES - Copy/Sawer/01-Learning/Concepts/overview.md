# Sawer Concepts: Polivoks Character and Signal Design

## Identity
Sawer is a subtractive synth modeled after the Soviet-era Polivoks. Its defining traits are the aggressive filter response, fast envelopes, and a voicing system that includes a main saw oscillator, sub oscillator, noise, and an 8-voice unison system. The manual emphasizes the analog-modeled filter, self-oscillation behavior, and a modulation matrix for performance routing. Use these as the core pillars of your research.

## Core Sound Architecture (Manual-Aligned)
- Main oscillator: saw shape with sync frequency control.
- Sub oscillator: level, phase, detune, and octave range controls.
- Noise oscillator: adds texture and grit.
- Filter: LP 12/24 dB, BP, HP modes, with resonance and keyboard/velocity tracking.
- Modulation: LFO, two ADSR envelopes (one assignable), modulation matrix.
- Unison: 8 voices, panning, detune, and Octaver.
- Effects: chorus, phaser, delay, reverb.
- Performance: arpeggiator, MIDI learn, modulation matrix.

## Why It Sounds the Way It Does
- The filter is intentionally gritty at higher resonance, which makes Sawer excel at aggressive bass and cutting leads.
- The sub oscillator phase and detune dramatically affect low-end weight and focus.
- Unison adds width but can smear low frequencies if overused.

## Research Goals
- Identify what resonance levels remain musical in bass vs lead contexts.
- Document how the Octaver expands harmonic range in unison.
- Create a reliable workflow for filter tracking and velocity response.
