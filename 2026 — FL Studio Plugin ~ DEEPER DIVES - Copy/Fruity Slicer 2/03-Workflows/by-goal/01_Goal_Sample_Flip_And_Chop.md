# Goal: Sample Flip & Chop - Fruity Slicer 2

**Time:** 10-20 minutes  
**Skill Level:** Intermediate  
**Result:** Completely rearranged sample creating new musical material

---

## The Sample Flip Philosophy

**Sample flip = taking existing audio and creating something entirely new through chopping and rearrangement.**

Classic examples:
- J Dilla chopping soul loops
- Kanye flipping Otis Redding
- Pete Rock rearranging jazz samples

---

## Complete Sample Flip Workflow

### Step 1: Source Selection (2-3 minutes)

**Best Samples to Flip:**
- **Soul/Funk loops** (rich instrumentation, musical phrases)
- **Jazz recordings** (complex harmony, improvisation)
- **Vocal performances** (hooks, ad-libs, phrases)
- **Orchestral pieces** (strings, horns, dramatic sections)

**What to Look For:**
- Musical phrases (2-8 bars ideal)
- Distinct sections (intro, verse, hook, breakdown)
- Rich harmonic content
- Good recording quality

---

### Step 2: Load and Analyze (2 minutes)

1. Load sample into Fruity Slicer 2
2. Listen multiple times, identify:
   - BPM (tap tempo)
   - Musical phrases
   - Interesting hits/stabs
   - Sections to emphasize

**Pro Tip:** Mark interesting moments mentally before slicing

---

### Step 3: Strategic Slicing (5 minutes)

**For Musical Content (Soul, Jazz, Vocal):**
- **Division:** 1/4 or 1/8 (match musical phrases)
- **Manual Slicing:** Mark chord changes, vocal phrases, horn hits
- **Goal:** Capture complete musical ideas per slice

**Workflow:**
1. Set correct BPM
2. Auto-Slice @ 1/4 or 1/8 (starting point)
3. Manually adjust slice markers to:
   - Start of chord changes
   - Beginning of vocal phrases
   - Horn stabs/hits
   - Drum hits (if rhythmic)

**Example: Soul Loop**
- Slice 1: Intro chord
- Slice 2: Vocal phrase "Ooh yeah"
- Slice 3: Horn stab
- Slice 4: Drum fill
- Slice 5: Next chord progression

---

### Step 4: Map and Test (2 minutes)

1. Slices auto-map to keyboard (C5, C#5, D5, etc.)
2. Play each slice individually
3. Identify:
   - **Anchor slices** (main hook, recognizable elements)
   - **Filler slices** (transitions, supporting elements)
   - **Accent slices** (one-shots, punctuation)

**Organization Tip:** Note which slices are most musical

---

### Step 5: Rearrangement Strategies (5-10 minutes)

#### Strategy 1: Phrase Flipping
**Take original phrase, reverse the order**

Original: Slice 1 → 2 → 3 → 4  
Flipped: Slice 4 → 3 → 2 → 1

**Result:** Familiar but fresh

---

#### Strategy 2: Stutter/Repeat
**Repeat key slices for emphasis**

Original: Slice 1 (once)  
Flipped: Slice 1 (4x in 1 bar)

**Result:** Rhythmic hook from static sample

**Common Pattern:**
```
Slice 1 on beat 1
Slice 1 on beat 1.5
Slice 1 on beat 2
Slice 1 on beat 2.5
(Creates stutter effect)
```

---

#### Strategy 3: Pitch Shifting
**Change pitch of individual slices**

1. Select slice in Piano Roll
2. Transpose up/down (±3-7 semitones common)
3. Create new melody from monophonic source

**Example:**
- Original slice at C5
- Copy to D5 (pitch up +2)
- Copy to F5 (pitch up +5)
- Creates chord/melody

---

#### Strategy 4: Rhythmic Reconstruction
**Use melodic slices rhythmically**

1. Take vocal or melodic slice
2. Trigger in time with drums (on snare, kick, etc.)
3. Creates percussive effect from tonal source

**Example:** Vocal "Yeah" on every snare hit

---

#### Strategy 5: Call-and-Response
**Alternate between two contrasting slices**

1. Identify two complementary slices
2. Alternate placement:
   - Slice 1 on beat 1
   - Slice 2 on beat 2
   - Slice 1 on beat 3
   - Slice 2 on beat 4

**Result:** Conversational, dynamic pattern

---

### Step 6: Processing Flipped Slices (3-5 minutes)

#### Essential Processing

**High-Pass Filter:**
- Remove muddy low-end (unless bass slice)
- HP at 200-300 Hz for clarity

**Reverb/Delay:**
- Add space and depth
- Short reverb (vintage plates)
- Slapback delay for character

**Saturation:**
- Analog warmth
- Tape/vinyl character
- Glues chopped slices together

**Compression:**
- Parallel compression for punch
- Gentle ratio (2:1 to 4:1)
- Maintains dynamics while adding density

## Sample Flip Recipes by Genre

### Boom Bap Soul Flip

**Source:** Soul/funk loop (2-4 bars)

**Slicing:**
- 1/8 division
- Manual adjustments at phrase boundaries

**Rearrangement:**
- Reverse chord progression
- Stutter vocal hooks
- Pitch shift horn stabs

**Processing:**
- Vinyl crackle layer
- Tape saturation
- Short plate reverb

**Vibe:** Warm, nostalgic, head-nodding

---

### Lo-Fi Jazz Flip

**Source:** Jazz piano or guitar loop

**Slicing:**
- 1/4 division (capture full chords)
- Manual at chord changes

**Rearrangement:**
- Slow, half-time feel
- Repeat key chords (looping)
- Pitch down -2 to -4 semitones (dark, smoky)

**Processing:**
- Heavy low-pass filter (dull, warm)
- Long reverb tail
- Bit-crushing (lo-fi texture)

**Vibe:** Chill, relaxed, introspective

---

### Trap Vocal Flip

**Source:** Acapella or vocal phrase

**Slicing:**
- Manual (syllable-based)
- Isolate key words/phrases

**Rearrangement:**
- Stutter on trap snare hits
- Pitch shift ±1 octave (dramatic)
- Rapid 1/32 rolls (glitchy)

**Processing:**
- Aggressive auto-tune
- Heavy reverb + delay
- Distortion/saturation (grit)

**Vibe:** Aggressive, modern, glitchy

## Advanced Flip Techniques

### Technique 1: Multi-Layering
**Stack multiple flipped versions**

1. Create Pattern 1 (original flip)
2. Duplicate to Pattern 2
3. Pitch shift Pattern 2 up +12 semitones
4. Play both simultaneously (octave layering)

**Result:** Thick, rich texture

---

### Technique 2: Slice Granulation
**Extreme chopping for texture**

1. Auto-Slice @ 1/32 (very fine)
2. Randomize slice order
3. Fast, glitchy playback
4. Creates granular texture

**Use:** Transitions, build-ups, experimental sections

---

### Technique 3: Envelope Shaping
**Sculpt slice timbre**

1. Select slice
2. Adjust ADSR:
   - Fast attack → Sharp, percussive
   - Slow attack → Smooth, swelling
   - Short decay → Tight, punchy
   - Long release → Sustained, atmospheric

**Example:** Turn piano chord into lush pad (slow attack, long release)

## Common Flip Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Over-Slicing**
- Too many slices = hard to rearrange
- Fix: Start with 1/4 or 1/8, add detail if needed

**Mistake 2: No Clear Hook**
- Random arrangement = unmemorable
- Fix: Identify and emphasize anchor slices

**Mistake 3: Ignoring Original Vibe**
- Flip loses character of source
- Fix: Preserve key elements (melody, vocal) while rearranging
