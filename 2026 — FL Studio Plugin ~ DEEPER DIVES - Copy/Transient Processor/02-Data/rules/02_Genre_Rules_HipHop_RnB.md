# Genre Rules: Hip-Hop & R&B

## The Trap Snare "Click" Rule
- **Rule:** Trap snares live or die by their transient.
- **Action:** Boost **Attack** by +4dB to +8dB. Simultaneously, reduce **Release** by -20%. This makes the snare "stiff" and "precise," which is the hallmark of the modern trap sound.

## 808 "Knock" Management
- **Rule:** When adding "knock" to an 808, always keep the **Split Freq** above **150Hz**.
- **Reason:** Transient processing on sub-frequencies (below 100Hz) creates rapid volume fluctuations that sound like "crackling" distortion. Protecting the sub allows the 808 to stay solid while the "click" part gets sharper.

## Sample "Drying" (The Lo-Fi Rule)
- **Rule:** Old soul samples often have too much "air" or room noise for a modern beat.
- **Action:** Reduce the **Release** until the snare hits in the sample sound "dry." This allows you to add your *own* high-quality reverb later, making the mix sound cleaner and more "expensive." [SRC: REPUTABLE-11]

## R&B Vocal "Softness"
- **Rule:** Sometimes digital vocal recordings are too "harsh" or "pokey."
- **Action:** Reduce **Attack** by -2dB or -3dB. This mimics the "slow" response of high-end vintage microphones and tube preamps, making the vocal feel "silky."

## Mono Bass Rule
- **Rule:** Transient processing does not affect stereo width, but it *can* emphasize side-panned transients.
- **Action:** Always check the **Histogram** to ensure the Attack boost is even on both sides (if working on a stereo file). If not, center the file before processing.
